     Finally Aunt Kate broke the spell by handing her the paring knife
(she had already washed the apples).
     "Oh yes," said Parepa-Roza coming to, "I guess I forgot what came
first".
     Aunt Kate was a shrud woman and knew when to keep her eyes as
well as her mouth shut.  She slipped into the dining room and began
setting the table.  This time for four.
     Parepa labored on, for it was real work, this being left with the
"ingredients" to combine her first salad and Aunt Kate away in another
room.  She wished she would come near once in a while just so she
could ask her if she was doing it right.  She was plucky enough
though and finished the job completely.
     When she was putting the lettuce on the plates she noticed there
were four and wondered if Aunt Katehadn't made a mistake.  She wondered
too, where Aunt Kate was, she hadn't heard a sound for a long time.
     Finally she came in the front door, calling out "Did my little
girl think I'd left her?"
     "Oh, what a pretty salad!  Won't Uncle George and Francis be
surprized when I tell them you made it?  Now how would you like to
run out in the garden and pick a few flowers for the center of our
dining table?"
     "Oh! that would be fune," but Parepa-Roza wondered why Aunt Kate
had such lovely flowers in the <u>back</u> yard.  Why not in the front yard
like the "Burnett girls" had theirs.  I wonder who Francis is, she
thought, as she picked far too many flowers for the small center bowl.
She's probably a neighbor's girl she thought.  I hope she's nice.
