                              CHAPTER III

     "I can't see how you expect to get by all summner on the small
amount of cash you have saved," said her anxious mother.  "You know
we can't help you any."

     "Don't worry, ⟦pencil-insert: Mother⟧ dear," she said kissing her.  "I know how to make
some.  I'll get along all right."  -   -   -   -   -

     We now find our Parepa-Roza back with us again, for on the Allen
Club register as well as on the record book of the University is the
name A. Parepa-Roza Wesley, Sparland, Illinois.

     She found lodging in a home across the street from the Club.  There
were five other girls rooming there and a lovely bunch they were.  Parepa
soon became a favorite at the Club as well as on the campus.  She
arranged with the Churches of near by towns to give dramatic entertain-⟦hyphen⟧
ments week ends for which she received one third the receipts at the
door.  This helped the financial end of affairs greatly.  Her ability
as an entertainer was soon recognized by the student body.  She was given
leading parts in pagents and plays as well as finding herself the
physical culture instructor's assistant.  She proved hers⟦strikeover: mistyped "le" (from "herslef") retyped over as "el"⟧f champion
bowler, a sport which she delighted in greatly.  She was never tempted
to sing even in the assembly.  No one would have suspected this young
girl had once possessed a voice that would rival any in the school.
She guarded her secret sacredly.  Never had she wanted to sing since
Aunt Kate's and (as she thought of him) Frances's deaths.

     Parepa was surrounded by admirers of the opposite sex continually.
These finally simmered down to one.  The smartest and most handsome
young fellow in school.  He had graduated with honors and had secured
a position as Principal of a small town high school.  He was there
through the summer session to "freshen up" on a few subjects.
