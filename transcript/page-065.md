                              CHAPTER XII

     Now ensued what proved to be the most irresponsible, delightful,
entirely healthful and enchanting year or two of Parep's life.  It was
a free, eager, venturesome, joyous life altogether and helped in the
development of the normal happy girl.
     There were many skating and coasting parties in the winter time.
These Parepa-Roza enjoyed to the limit.  Often some courageous young
gallant would grab her mittened hand and careen with her over the slick
cold ice.  Just as often some love-sick swain encircled her heavily
coated waist with his heavily coated arms as⟦strikeover: letter overtyped in "as"⟧ they coasted down the
long long hills on their big sleds.  It was a long and jolly ride from
the top of Spring Street Hill to the Monroe Street car tracks.  Some-⟦hyphen⟧
times they risked the more curved more exciting decent of old Seventh
Street Hill.
     Parepa-Roza was truly intoxicated with the joy and laughter
created by the very spirit and action these sports demanded.  One had
to keep on the move or freeze⟦strikeover: "f" overtyped on another letter⟧ to death.  She loved the loud high-pitched
yells of the merry coasters filling the chilly air as only irresponsible
youth can.
     There was always a noticable rush and scuffle amoung the boys to
"beat" sitting next back of Parepa-Roza on the long sleds.  She had no
favorites amoung them.  They were all alike - only Frances was different.
Sometimes she thought they held her on the sled a little tighter than
was necessary but she didn't mind - so much - being squeezed - a little.
There is safety in numbers you know, and there were always numbers.
