     Now Parepa was volunteering a whole sentence.  Not in answer to
a question but was actually making a statement telling Frances something
she wanted him to know.  Her face was all animation and she was motion-⟦hyphen⟧
ing to make it more impressive.  He was laughing with her and when she
had finished they both gave vent to a loud giggle.
     By this time all eyes in the car was upon them but they didn't seem
to know that even Uncle and Auntie were watching, so wrapped were they
in their own enjoyment.
     By the time they reached their destination these children were as
lively and alert as crickets.  Parepa seemed to have lost all her  re-⟦hyphen⟧
straint after the picnic lunch was over and gave herself up to the
game they were teaching her.  It was great fun and she proved herself
an apt scholar.
     "We'll come over real often, so you won't forget all you've learned",
said Uncle George as they waited for the street car to take them home.
     "You see I'll only be here two weeks unless I get a job, and maybe
if I do Papa and Mamma won't let me stay." said the girl thoughtfully.
"Let's look for a job tomorrow."
     "A job!  You a job!" laughted Frances.  "Gee Whiz, what could you
do?" and he roared with laughter.  "There's no cows to milk in Peoria."
     Parepa blushed again but she realized her inability to do much
else but milk cows.
     "Of course I'll have to start at the bottom of whatever I do, but
I can learn most anything I believe."
     The car came now and after they were seated the conversation went
back to "her job".  Frances couldn't imagine her working at anything.
He liked her extremely and wanted her to "play with" so to speak.
