                              CHAPTER XVI

     Surely this will be a good year  they would say on counting up
the days receipts.  It was as far as the return from the garden were
concerned but it was not a good crop year.  The early part of the season
was too wet; the later part too dry.
     It was the first day of oat harvesting.  All the family had helped
to get the old binder in running order.  Allie mended the drapers while
the boys ran errands for repairs⟦strikeover: "pa" overtyped in place⟧ as Johnie directed.  Helped, I said,
but to no avail.  It was so antiquated no wonder it felt it had served
its time and refused to work any longer.
     John inquired the price of a new one.  Two hundred and sixty-five
dollars was what one cost.  Impossible! at the price of oats and they
poor crop.  They would have to get along the best they could with the
old one.
     About mid-night Johnie was taken suddenly sick.  His suffering
increased rapidly in spite of all Allie could do for him.  She suspected
the nature of his trouble and called a specialist from Peoria.  After
asking some questions he demanded he be brought at once to Peoria to
the Methodist Hospital.  There must be no delay.  His condition was
serious.  In an hour he was in his room being examined by the Doctor,
having made the twenty mile trip in record time.
     Allie and Robert tired and worried from the toils and tribulations
