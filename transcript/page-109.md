     Uncle George came into the dining room just in time to eat with
them.
     "Morning, my beautiful ladies," he said.  "Glad to see you both so
wide awake this morning."
     "And how's our old man himself?" laughed Parepa.
     "Fine!  Fine!  Never been better," he lied.
     After breakfast Parepa went for a ride on her new wheel.  That
afternoon they had a glorious time in the park.  Parepa insisted they
have their lunch on the grass down by the rustic bridge.  Only she knew
why she liked it better than any other place in the park.  Just as
they were ready to seat themselves who should appear on the bridge but
Frances.
     At first he did not recognize them.  In fact did not think of it
being any one he knew.  He had hoped to have that very spot all to
himself that evening and when he noticed figures there he turned his
back and stood looking into the water as if deep in thought.  He was
health, life, beauty, and to Someone; love personified.  So engrossed
in thought was he that he did not notice the sweet blushing Parepa slip
up behind him and cover his eyes with her hands.
     "Who's there?" he cried.
     "Guess," she laughed.
     "No one but you," and he grabbed her hands and squeezed them in
his own.  "Where did you come from?  Out of heaven I should judge."
