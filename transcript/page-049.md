                              CHAPTER VIII

     After Parepa had left for the "candy store" the door bell gave a
loud ring and in came our hero, Frances.  After greetings were exchanged
and the evenings experiences talked over Frances became very sober and
thoughtful.  He seemed to have charged from the young boy to a man of
mature years and mind.
     "There's something I'd like to talk to you folks about now that
Parepa has gone and we have the room to ourselves."  Launching into
the subject at once he said, "No doubt you know all about Parepa's
circumstances and whether or not her folks are able to pay for the
education she should be getting at once.  It's not right to have her
spending one day in that candy shop.  I have only a short time to stay
around here and I have come to you for an ernest understanding."
     "I don't understand what you are talking about," said the bewildered
Uncle George.
     "Just this.  I don't want Parepa to know one word of this.  I am
well able to help her finish an uninterrupted education.  My plan is
to have her stay with you and I'll furnish all the money necessary.
I can see she is in good hands.  Can't we make some arrangements for
this?  There will be no obligations and I want no credit."
     "No!  Absolutely no!" said Mr. George Slay and Frances said no
more on the subject.
     "How long will you be in Peoria?" asked Mrs Slay timidly.
     "Only a week or so if things come around alright.  My guardian
had a great deal of business to attend to in America and it will depend
upon his success in getting it straightened up, when we leave.  We
will not come across again for two years."
     "What do you intend to make your lifes work?" asked Uncle George.
