     The oat harvest was over and threshing was well under way by the
time the Doctor thought best to operate.  The howled ⟦pencil: hauled⟧ Johnie's share of
the oats direct to the elevator from the threshing machine and sold
them for the low price of twenty-eight cents per bushel.  By the time
the twine and threshing bills were paid, only enough to pay up the
hospital and Doctor bills was left from their half of the entire oat
crop.  They tried to keep this fact from Johnie but his pointed questions
on the subject brought out the undisputed facts.  This caused an un-⟦hyphen⟧
favorable reaction and his condition became worse.
     When the day came for the operation it was with fear and trembling
that Allie waited for his return from the operating room.  She knew his
weakened condition better than the Doctors themselves.  There was born
in this woman something which could never die; a pity and compassion
for the weak the sick and the humble.  Though the world seemed ⟦pencil: she was⟧ powerless
to prevent it a mean resentment,too,possessed her now.  How could she
content herself to wait for the "silver lining" which she and Johnie
had so often watched for.
     Slowly it came into view.  First, a tiny edging of silver shown
from beneath the darkness.  Then day after day the cloud dissolved into
a gleam of shining metal.  Johnie is well again.
