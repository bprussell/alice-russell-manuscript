     "You know, my dear, if there is anything I can do to help you or
advise you I will be most happy to do so," he said.
     "Thank you so much, Uncle Will.  You have always been so kind to
me.  I'll never forget it.  Good night good kind Uncle," she crossed the
room to where he was sitting in his big sleepy-hollow chair and kissed
his bawled hea⟦strikeover: one character x-ed out⟧d.
     Uncle Will pulled her down on his lap and said teasingly, "Are
you sure you are not contemplating matrimony, child?"
     "May be," she laughed.  "All I need is the consent of the groom.
That's hard to get."  Then she sat looking down at the floor very sad.
     "Tell me about it Parepa, my little girl," and he drew her head
to his breast and tried to comfort her.  "Are you so much in love with
this young stranger?"
     "Stranger!  He is not a stranger.  I know him better than I know
myself," she sprang to her feet and said again, "Good night, dear Uncle
Will."
     After she had gone up the stairs Uncle Will sat for a  long time in
deep thought.  He saw her as she was that first day in his candy shop.
He recalled her birthday party.  The tender care the gave Aunt Kate.  Now
she was alone at eighteen years of age.  He resolved to have a talk with
this young Emmerson and find out for himself just what his intentions
toward the girl was.
     At last he went to bed greatly worked up over nothing that really
justified his concern.
