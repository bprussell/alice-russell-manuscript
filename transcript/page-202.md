home where flu⟦strikeover: stray letter x-ed out after "flu"⟧ was prevalent.  She finally secured the help of a
woman who came each day and confined her labors to the basement where
she washed,dried and ironed the many articles of clothing and bedding.
The little boys were desperately sick.  The Doctor and nurse spent
anxious hours over each one in turn.  Allie was continually waiting on
one or the other.  The young men didn't take it, neither did Allie.
     It was weeks before Johnie was able to be up.  Then he was so
weak it wasn't thought best for him to go down stairs for another ten
days.  The little boys gained faster and were now ready to start to
school.  They went one whole week when Richard developed an awful cough.
It was whooping cough.  In due time the other boys took it.  Weeks and
weeks of strangling, coughing, and restlessness followed.  Many sleepless
nights passed as she watched over them.
     It was spring now and the Doctor who had been keeping a close watch
on Johnie's improvement declared him able to go back to his duties
at the Avery Company.  The boys were a hard proposition these days.
They had been so sick and were weakened in strength and disposition from
the aggravating cough.
     Allie was tired out.  "I'm well, but so tired," she would tell
folks when they spoke of their alarm for her.  One bright morning in
May the boys were playing in the back yard when Allie thought Robert
looked flushed in the face.  He was crimson.  She brought him in and
although he declared he wasn't sick she called the Doctor.
     "Nothing else but the measles," he said casting a sympathetic look
at Allie.  "Must go to bed, young man," he said and remembering what a
laughing hilarious bunch of boys they were added, "And I'll dish you up
some giggle pills."
