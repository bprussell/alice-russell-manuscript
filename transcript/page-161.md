     Up to this moment the girl had sat quietly, his arms around her,
cheek against cheek as if afraid to stir for fear he might not go on
with his story.  Now she gave a start and a little cry of surprise.
Then as if her whole being was enveloped in that most precious of all
gifts - sympathy - she turned her lips to his and throwing her arms
around his neck kissed him tenderly.  "I love you -- you are mind⟦pencil: mine⟧ -
mine forever in spite of fate."
     Frances did not speak, he could not speak.  He hated himself,
hated all his blood, hated life,for the first time in his life he knew
the true meaning of hate.  But -- there was one thing he did not
hate.  That was Parepa.  Would he, should he keep on loving this human
thing in his arms?  Was it right?  Was it right?
     "Oh God help us!  Help us to know and understand what is right that
we should do."  He cried out in despair.
     "Let me answer that  prayer, Frances, dear," said "Bethany's Angel.
"Yes, for my sake - for your sake - And for his sake keep on loving me."
     Then straightening up and facing him kindly she seemed to have
survived the shock completely.  "Do you remember,that nearly one thousand
years ago,a Persian poet, Omar Khayyam wrote this poem:
          "Ah, Love could you and I with fate conspire,
               To grasp this sorry schemem of things entire;
          Would we not shatter it to bits and then,
               Remould it, nearer to the heart's desire?"
     "You, Frances, was your mother's love.  She and fate conspired.
They remoulded you 'nearer to the hearts desire'."
     "Yes, a real conspiracy in which I had no part," said Frances sadly.
