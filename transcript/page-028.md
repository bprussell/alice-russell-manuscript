     This was the first time in the boys life he was alone to make his
own friends and to do as he wanted with his time.  "He must be in by
ten o'clock" was his guardians strict orders to the old people with
whom he was trusted.  He seldom referred to his past and when he did
would halt his conversation as if he had told more than he intended to.
They learned this much - that he had crossed the ocean eight times.
How much time was spent on either side could only be guessed at.  That
he had never seen his father or mother was another fact secured on one
occasion.  "My father died more than eight months before I was born
and my mother three minutes before," he had said then shut up like a
clam.
     The next day Parepa was in deep thought.  She wondered how any
one could ever live without a father and mother.  Her sympathies were
aroused for this orphan boy and she determined to do all she could to
make him happy.  "He must have a big brother" she said to Aunt Kate,
"because he said that first night that Clem scrubbed his white ponies
every day."
     Aunt Kate explained to her that the "Clem" spoken of was probably
the stable man who looked after the horses.
     "And Aunt Kate, what country do pedigreed horses come from?  He
said his were all pedigreed." asked Parepa not caring to expose her
ignorance to him on that subject again.
     Aunt Kate explained as best she could and the subject was changed.
