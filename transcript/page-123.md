Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

all for her baby.  She wrote her wishes clearly and definitely.  There
could be no mistaking them.  She knew for sure as if she had some
special intimacy with the Gods of fate that she too could not survive the
ordeal of childbirth.  This did not seem to worry her.  She seemed to
feel it a fact not to be wrestled with and allowed no efforts made to
correct the heritage that was her's.  Often she would write for hours
messages to her unborn babe.  Messages to be opened at stated ages of
life.  She went so far as to send for me to come home.  It was her desire
I be her child's guardian.
     She addressed long letters with requests to be fulfilled to both
her nurse and myself.  She wanted her nurse to stay with her baby as she
had with her, etc.  Her mother had fallen heir to the family locket of
huge size strung on a thin chain of gold.  This in turn became the
possession of Annabell.  It was her idea to place certain truth requests,
advice, secrets and wishes together with her picture into this locket⟦unclear: ,⟧
take it to a jeweler and witness its sealing with a band of gold on which
was this inscription: . "To be opened on the fourteenth birthday of my
darling child," signed Anna Bell Emmerson.
     She urged her nurse and I to see that this locket was kept near
her baby at all times until it was old enough to wear it in safety.  He
was to be given this locket to play with at times and made to know it
was a most precious article.  After his fourteenth birthday he was to
wear it continually through life as a part of himself.
     As the time drew near my father learned of a wonderful physician .
A specialist in Glascoe that was highly recommended as being one surgeon
that could bring a child into the world safely and save the mother even
though conditions were mightly against her.
