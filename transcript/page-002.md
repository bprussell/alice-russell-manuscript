     This child liked to be alone with her thoughts and would work
hard and long if only she were promised a few hours to spend "as she
liked".  She always "liked" to be alone.  Never once would she choose
to visit her playmates if she had her way.
     "I can't understand the child" her kind mother would say, and
her father would add, "she's an odd one.  Smart as a whip but so
different from the others".
     She was a beautiful child despite her freckles, chubby form, and
curly redish hair.
     It was the custom of her parents to go each Saturday afternoon to
Chillicothe, a small town some miles distant, to do the family trading
and "take in the produce of the farm to exchange for necessities".
The rule was that one of the six children of the household was to go
with them, by turns, each week.
     Parepa-Roza seldom cared to go but would give her "turn" to an
older sister, whose hobby was going to town, whom she liked to get
off the place thus assuring her a better chance to do the things she
could "put over" better when this watchful bossy "big sister" was out
of hearing and seeing distance.
     Many times Parepa-Roza would watch until the old "rig" drawn by
two old gray horses was well out of sight over the "big hill" before
she would venture to try out some coveted "stunt" which had been
brewing in her mind since the last Saturday.
