"Oh No!  Look over there," and she pointed to the others.
     "Come, eat with us."
     "No thanks.  I don't want to impose on these good folks so much,"
he said starting to go.
     "Come on!  Come on!" shouted Uncle George.  "You must!"
     Parepa lead him by the hand and seated him between Aunt Kate and
herself.
     "This certainly is an unexpected pleasure," he said.  "I just
strolled out here to drink in some of the beauty going to waste."
     They were a jolly quartet laughing and singing as merrily as if
they were all in their teens.  Frances carried the lunch basket up the
hill and they sat down on a pavilion seat to rest before starting home.
     Frances excused himself and went inside, coming out with a waiter
carrying four dishes of ice cream.  In about an hour a carriage drove
up to the pavilion and Frances informed them he had 'phoned for it
when he was in for the cream.
     "That's fine of you," said U⟦unclear: n⟧cle George.  "I'm so tired I wondered
how I was going to make it to the car."
     "I thought that pull up the hill after lunch was tiresome for you
both," he said and hated  himself for letting them know he had noticed
it.
     "Guess we'll have to admit we aren't as young as we use to be
won't we, mother?" and Uncle George chucked her under the chin.
     When they reached home Frances wouldn't go in, saying he'd see them
tomorrow.
     "Oh, hire a bicycle and we'll take a spin in the country," begged
Parepa.
