Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

                          CHAPTER XXII

     That afternoon found two young people sitting on a slope of the
hill in a secluded spot of the park, thoroughly oblivious  of the sighing
of the breeze, the song of the birds, the beauty of the flowers, the
trickling of the waters, or the distant strains of music wafting over the
hill.  These two lovers were alone.  It was heavenly to be together;
neither could ask more.  They dreamed silently as if words were frail
instruments of expression.  Why bother with them?  Just to sit with hands
clasped, heads together, hearts beating as one.  Why couldn't it always
be this?  The shadows were falling now, and the.darkness seemed to beckon
confidence.
     "Parepa, dar, won't you listen to my story now?  I <u>must</u> tell you
for your sake and my sake.  Come, darling, won't you listen?"  His voice
was trembling.
     "Please, oh please," she said, "Frances my lover, I am so happy
why let your secret worry you?  It doesn't worry me.  Let's both forget
there is a secret," and she pulled his face down to hers and pressed her
lips to his,in a long lingering kiss⟦pencil-delete⟧.

     Again he pulled the large flat locket from his breast and begged,
"Darling, I can never let you kiss me like  that again until you know
there is to know - then you shall be the judge.  It's because I love you
so much, dearest, that I insist on you hearing me through.  You are
afraid to hear and I am afraid to have you hear, but never-the-less you
must, or we must part forever."
     "Oh! Frances, we must go home.  I'm getting so sick, <u>so sick</u>,"
she cried.  I feel as if the very life has gone out of me.  I don't know
what is the matter.
