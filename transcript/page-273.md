Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

     "Indeed it does.  The night at the Fey Hotel in old Peoria and then
the theatre.  Could I ever forget Uncle Tom?  Ha! Ha!"  They both laughed
heartily.  "I hope you won't cry all over your pretty pink gown," he
teased.

     "Poor Uncle George!  How he tried to comfort me," she laughed.

     "Yes, and how I wished I was in his place going home; holding you
in his arms.  And do you remember the night on the river, and old Glen
Oak park?  And - you, and the sunsets?  I live them over and over.
Parepa, dearest, its been the very life of me."  He rang for the next
course.

     As they sat waiting, Parepa opened the jewel box.  There was a
necklace, bracelet, broach, and two rings all of platinum set with
diamonds.  They were of unusual design, and when Parepa raved over their
delicate filigree.  Frances said, "Do you recognize any ones name
there?"

     Parepa studied the pattern for some time.  "Yes," she cried, "Here
is my name woven into the necklace and in the bracelet too."

     "Yes, and into both rings and the broach," he added.  Together they
deciphered⟦strikeover: second letter overtyped on another letter⟧ them.

     "Now can you find my name any place?" he said, as if they were
working over a puzzle.

     "Sure enough!  Just as plain as day!" she said.  "After one gets
use to it,  It's woven right into mine," she said excitedly.

     "That was the way I thought my life would be too when I  drew that
design," he said.

     "You drew it!  Why Frances you are an artist.  They are simply
wonderful!" and she held them up in her hand.  "Just see them sparkle
and laugh."
