                              CHAPTER VIII

     The Doctor grew serious as soon as he examined him.  "You may
as well know it will be necessary for you to be in bed for sometime.
You have a serious case of Pericarditis - that is, an inflammation of
the membrane that surrounds the heart - there is also considerable
fluid gathered there.  You must be kept very quiet," he added.

     Then turning to Allie asked, "Do you think you can take care of
him or shall I call for a nurse?"

     "Oh, no not a nurse.  Let her take care of me.  I want her too."
pleaded Johnie, and the Doctor consented.

     "I'll do my best Doctor but if you think I'm not capable we'll get
a nurse," and she smiled her most sincere smile at her pale husband
laying before her.

     "You will be all right if you can stand it.  It is going to take
strenuous treatment for some time, Mrs Bradford.  The ice cap must be
kept over his heart, a complete record kept of everything going in and
coming out of his body, and accurate temperature and resperation report
kept, his medicine must be given as directed day and night without a
miss, beside the many other duties that are bound to present themselves."
The Doctor looked at Allie as if he expected her to turn the job over
to a nurse but he was mistaken.

     "I can do all that I'm sure," she said, and was rewarded by the
relieved expression on her husband's face.

     "I'm so glad you will try, Allie," he said weakly as the Doctor
began preparing the medicines.  He wrote down the directions very
plainly.  Left her a chart for reports, an ice cap and thermometer for
her use and left with a feeling of confidence in her ability to care
for her patient.
