     Uncle Will spoke for he and Aunt Minnie.  "You can imagine my
surprize when brother George came to me and told me that Parepa (as we
all like to call her) wanted a job.  We both laughed about it and
decided to give her a try out at the candy store.  I didn't think she
would stay.  Thought she would get homesick and leave for the country
in a few days.  However, before the first day was over I decided that
if I could help it she would never leave.  That first day she made her-⟦hyphen⟧
self indispensable by building a most beautiful rainbow from colored
candies in one of the show cases.  I'll never forget the look of
truimph on her face when she showed it to me.  I appreciated her love
for color and realized in her a wonderful asset to our business.  She
has never disappointed me.  We congratulate you, Parepa, and wish you
many, many more happy birthdays.
     After the meat course was cleared, Mrs Breen told of the surprizes
she had received while teaching Parepa.  "I might say she taught me
nothing was too hard for her to undertake.  Her sweet disposition always
made me feel as if I had associated with ethereal beings.  Congratulations
my dear," and she made a low bow to Parepa.
     The usual "Thank You" was Parepa's only response.
     Mr. Wheeler stood up and glancing around the table mischieveously
said:  "You asked me to tell you what my first impressions were of this
young lady.  Well, the first time I saw her she stood at the top of
these stairs in her nightie.  She impressed me as a ten-year old.
Surely no older.  She looked sweet enough to kiss.  Her hair hung over
her shoulders and down her back reminding me of a picture of an angel
I once saw.  The next time I saw her was on the stage at the Opera House.
