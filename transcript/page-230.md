Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

     "I know it Johnie, and I have lost a good many night's sleep over
this very thing, but lets not spoil the sunset by worrying now.  Some
way that sum set brings memories of days gone by.  Oh, yes, I remember!"
then she sat quietly thinking.  It was the same sunset that she had
raved over nearly thirty years ago.  The one she and Frances had witnessed
as they road their steeds home from old Glen Oak that evening after
pledging their love for each other.  What a mess she had made of life.
After all, would it have been better had she never been a real mother?
What had she to offer these three fine fellows that call her mother?
No past, no present, and no future.  She loved each one better than her
life; yet she seemed so helpless to do the things she would like to do
for them.
     The sun had sank behind the horizon now and Johnie noticing the
tears that just would come  in her eyes said, "Come in now, dearie, its
getting chilly out."  Together they entered the house where the boys
were "washing up" for supper.  They were a happy bunch.  Laughing,
teasing and singing.  From all appearances they were as happy as kings.
"I guess it's all right, I'm a mother," Allie said to herself.
     At the table they related the days happenings as enthusiastically
as if they had been the main actor in a foot ball game.  Yes, theirs
was a simple life but a true one.  They were all smart and quick to
learn from life's school.  All unknowingly they were storing up rare
instruments for future use that could not be purchased on the market.
Each was talented with some rare gift of nature.  The sweet voice
which this mother had lost was recovered by each of her sons.  She
watched their progress year after year and had prayed for the time to
come when her boys should have a chance to cultivate their gifts.
