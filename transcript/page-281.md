Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

from Parepa's.  It was a simple form of arrangement yet it seemed
complete in every detail.  Parepa wondered at the matter-of-fact way
they discussed affairs pertaining to Frances's departure from this life.

     "Now, I want this to be plainly understood by all here,  Parepa,
I mean Mrs Bradford, here, is right now the owner and full possessor
of this estate and everything, omitting nothing, that I have owned.  I
have given it all to her.  Is that plain to you all?" asked Frances.

     "I suppose you mean all household goods, too," said Jimmy.

     "Yes, everything.  I have them all catalogued in a book which I
will give to her today.  She can do just as she pleases with everything.
You three will be responsible for their care the same as you have been
these past five years, according to the agreements," Frances answered.
"You may go now," and she sank back in her pillows as if weary.

     "You are so good Frances.  Heaven is right on earth where you are,"
and Parepa rose and walked to her bedside,taking her hand inhers she
said,"I must let you rest now, darling.  I want you to feel good this
afternoon.  I have a surprize for you,"  Kissing her she said, "Now don't
ask me to stay longer.  I know you are tired.  I'll see you at three
this afternoon; until then, good bye," and she turned and blew a kiss
to her from her hand.

     After Parepa read the letter from home, she was sitting at her
writing desk ready to answer it when she heard the knocker on the door.

     Daddy Wheeler entered carrying the catalogue of articles in the
household.  "Tomorrow we will start going over the house and viewing
each thing so you will remember them.  There are pictures of most of
them,"  he said.  "Here are the servants agreements," he said laying
them on the table.  "Frances had a serious time last night," he added.
