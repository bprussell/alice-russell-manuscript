Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

     He was asking now, nearly everyday, if the big hogs weren't ready
to ship.  The first of February had come.  It was necessary to find a
hired  man to look after the farming.  There was no money in the bank.
The nurse and specialists had to be paid - what was she going to do?

     When the two weeks were up Parepa insisted she was able and willing
to take up her duties as nurse again.  She really had gotten no rest.
She had more time to worry about other things.  She succeeded in con-⟦hyphen⟧
vincing them that she was rested so it was decided to let the nurse
go.

     It was a continual fight from then on to master the awful disease
that lurked in his body but on the fourth of July he was permitted to
sit for a short time in a chair.  It was over seven months since he
had done this.

     They were so happy again.  Now he would get well.  He would be
him old self again.  The Doctor bills and everything else would be paid
some day and they would begin all over again.

     On the seventeenth of August (Allie's birthday) he was able to
take his first walk to the barn.  The crops looked good.  He has been
told of the live stock losses so was prepared to see the hog pens
empty.  He was a brave man in more ways than one.  He faced the future
squarely.  "We will make it all right, Allie," he said as he kissed
her hand.  "It's been hard and lonesome for you but you've been wonderful
and some day you'll be proud of me.  We'll get along.  You see if we
don't."
