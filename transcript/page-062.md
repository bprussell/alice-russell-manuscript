     "Some Beads?"
     "No, No, No.  You'll never guess it."
     "What in the world can it be, Uncle George?"
     "What would you say if I'd tell you we have found an elocution
teacher for you?"
     "Oh! Oh!" she said jumping up and clapping her hands.  "Really!
Where is she?"
     "Well, first you will have to decide whether or not you want to
pay the price.  It comes pretty high, Parepa, and Aunt Kate and I can't
afford to pay for the lessons much as we would like to."
     "I'm afraid I won't have time to study, with my music."
     "Your Uncle Will and I have settled the time question," spoke⟦strikeover: final letter overtyped⟧ up
Aunt Kate.  "You can take your lessons from seven-forty-five to eight
forty-five two mornings a week.  It is about a ten minutes walk to the
shop from her studio.  It will cost you four dollars a week.  That will
only leave you one dollar to yourself."
     "Do you think I could buy my clothes on that," she said wistfully.
     "No, but if you will be satisfied to wear what I can make over
for you I believe we can make a go of it." Aunt Kate replied.
     "I'd like to much to try anyway." said the delighted girl. "Maybe
some day I can pay you good folks back for all you are doing for me.
Oh, I'm so happy!" and she kissed one and then the other until there
was no doubt of her love for them.
