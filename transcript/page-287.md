                          CHAPTER XXVII

     That night Parepa slept like a child.  At nine o'clock the next
morning a tap on the door and her maid announced Daddy Wheeler.  He
looked composed and well groomed.  Parepa thought perhaps he was going
to suggest a shopping trip to the City this beautiful morning.  He
greeted her with a smile and a kiss.

     "Are you as fine as the day my dear?" he asked drawing her close
in his arms.

     "Yes, Daddy, I wouldn't know how to act if I felt any better,"
she laughed.

     "That's good," and helping her to a chair he drew another closer.
Reaching for her hand he said, "Frances left us this morning, Parepa.
She has gone to her God.  All is well.  All is well."

     Parepa could only stare at this wonderfully poised old man.  This
man who was resignation personffied.  Could she control herself as
this fine example before her?  Yes, she would.  Frances would want her
too.  It was her way.  She must live up to the standard set by this
bereft old gentleman.  Strangely enough Parepa had no desire to weep
again.

     The plans were made and every wish was carried out.

     As Parepa-Roza took her place beside Daddy Wheeler, and the little
procession moved slowly from the sobered "Kashema" the setting sun
tinted the skies with a thousand hues of red, gold, and purple.  Then
the night - the desert moon and stars swing like hanging lamps - so near
to the earth that they seemed almost to touch the thorny fingers of
the stately old mountains that stood as watchmen of the dunes.
