                              CHAPTER XXII

     As they neared the limousine she recognized the calmly face of
Daddy Wheeler.  He was standing straight and tall beside the car looking
toward them.  He removed his hat and waved at her.  When she came nearer
he met her with out stretched arms.  He kissed her cheek and greeted
her with warm words of welcome.  There was nothing offensive about this
grand old man in his eighties.
     He was the picture of good health and right living, well preserved
in every way.  His hand clasp was strong and his cheery words, "Welcome
my daughter," would have been a shock to Parepa had they come from
any one else.  After the driver assisted them to their seat in the car,
Mr. Wheeler said, "You better give him your baggage checks and he will
get it for you."
     Parepa blushed as she told him she had none.  "This is all I
brought.  I can only stay a few days you know."  The subject was dropped
but she thought Daddy looked amused.As they rode through the City of
Tucson Allie thought she never saw so much beauty.  Daddy Wheeler keenly
noted her surprize and delight as he pointed out to her many interesting
places.  Old missions, prehistoric ruins, the great Pioneer Hotel, the
Pepper Tree Inn, the Eucalyptus trees, the towering peaks of the Santa
Ritas and Mesa Verde Range. And the sunshine!  Was she to become a Sun
Worshiper too?  They rode for several miles in the country, then all
at once on a desert knoll there appeared an impressive structure of
Spanish architecture, conforming to no very definite school of design.
     She knew without being told that this was Frances's home.
     "There is it," she said.  "I know it is."
