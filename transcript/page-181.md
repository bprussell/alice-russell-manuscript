     When Parepa reached her room she threw herself on her bed and
wept bitter tears.  She didn't know why she wept.  She couldn't explain
her feelings to even her own satisfaction.  She knew she was madely in
love, but with whom?  She was afraid it was Johnie.  She wasn't sure -
for hours she tossed on her bed.  Why was he - how could he be so
patient - so willing to wait for her decision.  Why didn't he urge her -
force her - make her see his worthiness.  Finally sleep overcome her
and she lay as a punished child, sobbing as she slept.

     In another room of that same Hotel, a young man lay awake feeling
happy in the thought he was so near to the girl he loved.  She was glad
he had come - she had said so - tomorrow we will have a good visit.  Will
she love me sometime?  Of course, she will - I can wait.  She must have
her own time - I'll not coax her.  Someday she will be  my wife - I
must bide my time.  He dropped to⟦strikeover: double-struck⟧ sleep in a peaceful restful confident
state of mind that is the possession of only such honest fair minded men
as Johnie Bradford.

     The next morning at ten o'clock two young people met with the
spirit of the day ringing in their ears.  Horns and whistles blowing,
flags flying, drums beating, fire crackers deafening them.  Such crowds!
It certainly was no place for confidence,  No place for them.

     They lunched at noon, then took a street car to its farthorest
destination where they left it and strolled far out into a woods.  Allie
loved this - she always did.  So did Johnie - this being alone with the
girl of his heart, so far from everyone else.  They chatted along,
laughing and joking as they went until they reached a winding stream.
