                                                                    79

Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

Mr. and Mrs. George Slay to whom she gives all credit for her success.
     Miss Alice Parepa-Roza Wesley of the Sparland vicinity came to
make her home with her Uncle  and Aunt just two years ago.  Mrs Slay
having been before her marriage famous in grand Opera recognized in
this slip of a girl the possibilities which she proved so positively
were hers.  Mrs Slay taught her music and placed her under the tutorage
of Mrs Rose Mendenhall Breen dramatic instructor.  The child made such
startling advancement that her teacher has trusted her with evenings
entertainment.
     She is soloist in one of the large Churches of this city.  Though
her voice is still childish it is all the more appealing.  In refusing
the astonishing offer made her by L.M. Parker, Manager, Miss Wesley
stated that she was not desirous of a stage career and that no flattering
offer would tempt her to follow the footlights."
     Mr. Slay put down the paper and stared at Parepa.  "Is it possible
you are so senseable, little one?  Such a success as you were last
night, would turn any girls head."
     "Not our girl - remember, George dear, she is an exception to
the rule."  Smiled Aunt Kate so proud she could hardly contain herself.
     "Dear Auntie and Uncle, do you think I could be so heartless as
to leave you now?  Remember you were the making of me.  I'll never
forget.  Now let's get the dishes done and ready for Church."
     When she entered the choir loft she smiled at Frances who was
surrounded by a group of young men and women.
     He came over to her and whispered, "Saw your name in the paper
this morning."
     "Strange," she answered smiling, "Uncle and Auntie saw it too."
