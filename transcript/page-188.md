                              CHAPTER VII

     June 21st, 1905, was a day long looked forward too and its setting
sun witnessed the uniting of two hearts and lives - for better or for
worse - for richer or for poorer - in sickness and in health until
death do them part.

     This happy couple met and overcome the many unexpected matrimonial
intricacies which are bound to arise in all well regulated families.
By the end of the third year they were nearly out of debt and owned a
fair equipment both of household furnishings and farm machinery.  Allie
was fortunate in winning a piano in a contest which she made good use of.
However, she never sang.  They had accumulated considerable live stock
of which they were justly proud.  Each was interested in everything that
concerned the other.  Both were working hard and enjoying every bit of
it.

     Fall came - the ripened corn hung from its  husks on the tall stalks
in the fields as though beckoning the harvester to gather them in.
Allie loved to help her swarthy companion pile high in the wagon the
golden ears.  They visited and planned as they worked.  One day Allie
was sure there was something worrying Johnie.  He wasn't himself.  What
could be on his mind?  She wouldn't ask him.  He would tell her soon.
