Submitted by:

Mt. Hawley Road

                              CHAPTER XXIV

     "After we had landed and comfortable quarters were located I lived
for months as one in a trance.  Seeing nothing, hearing nothing, knowing
nothing but my baby.  The Doctors sailed on the first ship for America.
It was an ill fated ship and sank - both Doctors were drowned.  This
sealed the news of the birth of a living child and its identity was lost.
     I forgot to tell you that only those of our party knew its
personel or the purpose. of this voyage.  The general opinion of the
passengers on the ship was that Anna Bell and I were husband and wife.
Our party kept its own counsel and made no new acquaintances.  The six
of us stayed close to our reservation on the ship.  Our meals were
served in our apartment and the every wish of our party was cattered to
at the ring of a bell.  None of us were on a pleasure trip.  The Doctor's
centered all their thoughts in study while the rest of us tried to make
the time pass as pleasantly as possible for Anna Bell.  She having the
highest spirits in the party.
     The nurse, Frances, and I lost ourselves in Great Britian for
several years.  As my baby grew to childhood I could see the features of
his mother developing rapidly.  He was the same little child my father
had reared so carefully in our home.  Those snappy dark eyes, curly
black hair, ruddy complexion, lithe body, charming ways, and lovable
disposition.
     Of course, this child must be raised a real boy, no sissy ways
for him.  His pleasures, his exercise, his associates must be of the
kind that make for the better man.  With this end in view I have followed
a carefully planned schedule all these years.  You can see what the
result has been.  A bright, alert, well proportioned and well balanced
youngster, twenty years old the same day Parepa was sixteen."
