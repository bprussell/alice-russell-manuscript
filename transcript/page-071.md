Our little girl will be of age.  I hadn't thought of that before.  Well

to make their own living.  Just think, George, in two years you and I
have made it possible for this dear child to be what she alone knew
she could be - a real actress - a real song bird."
     "Yes, and there is a real surprize in store for this young chap
when he sees her tonight", laughed Mr. Slay.  "Let's having her sitting
at the piano singing when he comes.  You and she singing a duet and
I'll let him in."
     "Fine!  We'll do that and then we will all see the fun.  Well, I
must get us a bite to eat now," and she hurried to the kitchen all
excited.
     Mr. Slay left to himself sat to thinking over the day he brought
Parepa from her country home in that old tea wagon.  He could see the
cunning freckles on her nose as she turned her face to his and said,
"Yes, and from now on don't you ever forget it, I'm sixteen years old."
And I promised her I'd never tell,he mused.  And I never have, not
even the best friend the child could ever have, my wife.  I wonder if
I have done wrong.  Frances too, thinks she will be of age.  I believe
the girl believes it herself.  Anyway she has never referred to it to
me.
     When Parepa came home she went about her usual performances
innocent of the great surprize in store for her.
     At the table that evening Uncle George teased, "Come now, Peppy,
tell your old Uncle when the young Lochenvar is coming?"
     "Oh, some sweet day by and by," sang Parepa.  "Don't know exactly.
Will let you know in time to get your "boiled" shirt on," she laughed.
