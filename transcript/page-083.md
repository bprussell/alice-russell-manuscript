                              CHAPTER XVII

     Parepa spent the afternoon and evening resting.  Reaction came in
the form of tired mind.
     "I just want to be left alone to sleep and rest," she told Aunt
Kate.  This wise woman understood and kissing her warm cheek closed her
bedroom door and crept quietly down the steps to her husband who was
sitting on the sofa waiting for her to come to him.  There seemed to
be a completed understanding between these two people.  One always
knew just what the other expected of them and always did it⟦unclear: l⟧  Just them
Uncle George wanted his wife to kiss him and she did.  Then she slipped
down beside him real close and his arm closed around her.  Here they
were again, nothing to say, just happy in each other's company.  Once
in a while Uncle George's hand would carress Aunt Kate's hair.  Once
in a while Aunt Kate would squeeze Uncle George's hand.  And once in
a while two pair of eyes would meet and speak for two tongues which
were numb.  Such was the mutual understanding of this devoted couple.
     Monday morning found Parepa up and coming as usual.  She went
through her lessons as earnestly as if they were her first ones.
Success did not mean the end for her - only the beginning.
     That noon when Uncle Will and she were seated at lunch he became
confidential.  "We are all so proud of you, Parepa," he said, "And
you refusing to go on the stage gives me more pleasure than your success.
That showed the real metal in you."
