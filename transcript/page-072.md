                                                                    70
Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

     "Let's see - how old is that young scamp now, 'bout eighteen,
eh?" he quized.
     "I guess so, but don't you know I can't remember of his ever
telling us how old he was.  Do you, Aunt Kate?" she asked turning to her
for an answer.
     "Only that when I went over to ask Frances to come to supper with
us the night you brought Parepa home with you the old lady told me
she thought he was close to sixteen.  Don't know how she knew.  Just
guessed it maybe," she said.
     Parepa glanced sideways at Uncle George and he at her.  Both knew
well what the other was thinking.  Then she said, "Do you remember how
that Mrs. Jackson guessed me sixteen that day I was in the tea wagon,
Uncle George?" she smiled.
     "I'll never forget it.  Her girl Ruth was sixteen, too, I believe,"
he chuckled.
     "And our little tea wagon darling is soon going to be of age.
Eighteen years old, Parepa, in just one more month.  Then you will be
your own boss.  Think of it!  Not our little girl any more.  Oh!
Parepa, Dear little girl, it don't seem possible," and Aunt Kate really⟦strikeover: final letter overtyped⟧
felt a tear trickling down her cheek.
     "Got a  long way to go yet before you are as old as we'ens,"
laughed Uncle George.
     "Yes, or as wise either, or as good to everybody," and she jumped
up and kissed one and then the other.  "I'm so happy I can hardly stand
it and I don't know why.  I'm sure it's not my approaching birthday
because that seems awfully old for one so young," she laughed.
     Only Uncle George knew what she was thinking.
