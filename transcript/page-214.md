     It was ⟦pencil-insert: a⟧ busy springtime both inside and out at the Bradford home.
Everyone in the family set about cleaning up the house and yards.  Wagon
load after wagon load of trash was hawled away.  The landlord consented
to paying for the window glass⟦strikeover: final letters overtyped⟧ for the four big front rooms up stairs
and down.  He also had the plaster patched on the walls and ceiling
where it was needed.  The old rickety window shutters were removed
entirely.  Every room was scrubbed and washed with scalding water and
soap.  Lye was sometimes necessary.  It was thoroughly aired until no
one to see it now would have suspected it had been the haven of rest
and birth place of the feathered tribe for years.
     Allie enjoyed every minute of the work.  She liked to see the grain
of the beautiful woodwork appear from beneath the years accumulation
of dust and dirt as she plied her wash cloth back and forth again and
again over a small surface of window casing or door panel.  She was
thankful this house had been well built and of finest material.  Allie
appreciated this fact.
     It took nerve to attack a job of this kind.  Only one with the gift
of foresight to see ahead the reward of their labors would have attempted
it.
