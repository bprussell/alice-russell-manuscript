made.
     On February twenty-fifth an old neighbor from the country called
on Johnie and advised him to make sure he was going to get the mortgage
money.  He told how the owner of the farm had lost everything and how
his creditors were closing in on him from all sides, "The onlyway
you'll ever get anything out of your second mortgage will be to take
the farm back," he said.
     This proved to be the case.  Not only did he have to take the place
back but there were back taxes, back interest on the first twelve
thousand mortgage and lots of tedious and expensive legal transactions
to be paid for.  It was impossible to go on with the Colorado land deal.
