                              CHAPTER V

     The next day was Sunday and Parepa-Roza asked what Church and
Sunday School they went to.
     "Well, Missie, we haven't gone to Church for a long time, but
we'll go wherever you want to." said Uncle George.  "There's plenty of
them near here.  Let's see, there's an Episcopal, Baptist, Universalist,
Congregational, Methodist, Catholic, Christian ⟦unclear: Lutheran⟧, and Presbyterian
all within walking distance.  Take your choice". he said smiling.
     "Let's go to the Congregational then," and that was settled once
and for all for Auntie and Uncle found themselves sitting up straight
in a pew every Sunday morning and found themselves really enjoying the
sermons and music
     "Isn't it queer how we ever got out of the habit of going to
Church?" said Aunt Kate one Sunday after enjoying an especially interest-⟦hyphen⟧
ing sermon.
     "Guess we just quit and never had any one to start us in again
until our girl here came our way", returned Uncle George and he took
Parepa's chin in his hand and turned her face to his. "Do you know you
have a great responsibility in this family, my dear, keeping your old
Auntie and Uncle going straight."
     Parepa only laughed.  This was one of her charms, but she didn't
realize it.  When she laughed she displayed a beautiful row of pearly
teeth, her cheeks dimpled, her eyes flashed, while the laugh itself was
a musical ripple which captivated its hearers.  When she didn't know
what to say, as in this instance, her laugh came to the rescue and was
all that was necessary.
