     "Did she suffer terribly?" asked Parepa.

     "No, she never suffers.  Just collapses and it takes strenuous
treatment by the nurses to bring her around again.  We never know what
a day will bring forth," he added thoughtfully.  "Did I ever tell you
her wishes about her burial, Parepa?"  "You know Parepa, Frances has
a true religion all her own.  Not one influenced by a creed or order.
It is such a live thing to her that all else is dominated by it.  It
is the back ground that serves and supports her in all times of test or
trial.  Her love for the Supreme Being, her Father, her Helper, is a
most natural thing for her.  He understands her frail creation.  In
silence she goes to him and unburdens her longings and becomes refreshed.

     Her "home made" religion is clothes with love.  It is her property,
and was created out of beauty, kindliness, and regard for the rights of
others.  I have always noticed that her own burdens disappear as she
lightens the burdens of others."

     "No Daddy, you didn't tell me.  Has she planned that too?" she asked.

     "Yes indeed.  They are very simple.  She wants to be taken to a
little church yard in the country about fifty miles from here, and wants
no service of any kind.  She says she and her God understand each other
and that is all that is necessary.  I have promised to carry out her
wishes to the letter.  She wants no procession - only her servants and
I to follow her.  There must be no publicity.  These three Johnsons and
I were all that knew her wishes.  She wants to be buried at night and
hopes it will be moonlight.  I suppose that sounds inchristian to you,
but I have a great deal of respect for her wishes.  She has a reason for
