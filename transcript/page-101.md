Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

     She chattered along right friendly for several miles, then she
seemed to prefer silence on my part as well as her own.  She was deep
in thought but I couldn't penetrate her mind.  I just supposed she was
getting homesick and wished she hadn't come.  Finally I ventured to say,
'I wish you were our little girl.  Aunt Kate and I would just love to
have you live with us always', and to my surprize she turned her shining
face up to mine and said: "Oh, Uncle George, do you really mean it?
Would you really keep me?'  I had not looked for this and I hardly
knew what to say.  Then the little minx begged, 'and then can I work?'.
I laughed aloud.  Her work! what could she do?  As we road along she
grew real talkative on the work proposition and confessed she had
hoped she could work out and earn some money to study on.  Young as she
was she had a business head on her as old as her Uncle beside her.  She
knew it took money to make dreams come true and she was willing to
earn it.  We all know the success she has won.  "Now, Mrs Slay tell
them what you thought of her when you found I had brought her bag and
baggage home to you."
     Mrs Slay laughed as she said:  "Iwas ⟦unclear: so, typed above the line with caret⟧ happy I could have taken the

day I had seen since the death of our own little Maude who would have
been eighteen now.  Parepa has been the life of us both and we are so
proud of her.  She has done us both great honor by making the best of
the opportunities made possible by us.  Now our little girl is eighteen -
of age - her own boss - Can you imagine that?  Congratulations darling,"
and she sank down into her chair, a tear in her eye.
