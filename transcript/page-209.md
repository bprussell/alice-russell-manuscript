                              CHAPTER XIII

     They moved onto the farm and set about their new duties with a
heavy heart but with a determination to make good if such a thing be
possible, handicapped as they were.
     The little boys were overjoyed.  They got a great thrill out of
caring for the pigs, calves and chickens.  Their many little helpful
steps made the burden of chore time lighter for their parents.
     It was such fun going to the timber for the cows.  Their father
bought a small pony for them and what sport it was to play cow boy.
John went in for dairy products while Allie raised poultry and flowers -
Dahlias being her specialty.  Selling the bulbs in the spring through
advertizing.  She found great pleasure in the study of plants.  No
time was wasted by any member of this family.  Everyone was made to
feel responsible for the performance of certain duties and no shirking
was countenanced no matter how slight.
     There was plenty of time for good healthy sport and these three
brothers succeeded in having their share of fun together.  What one
couldn't think of another could.  Thus, they grew into chubby healthy
fellows.  Their religious training was not neglected by any means.
Every Sunday found the Bradford family in their pew at the Edelstein
Congregational Church.
     Four years passed.  It had taken every ounce of effort and a lot
of careful scheming to make the interest each year.  It was discouraging

prices did the same thing, but everything the farmer needed to buy was
