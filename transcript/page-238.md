     "Now maybe⟦strikeover: "ay" overtyped; reads like "mgybe"⟧ I can talk to my family," she said seating herself at her
place at the table.  Her eyes fell on the locket outlined on the cloth.
Quickly she turned away.  Why should that haunt her so tonight.  "Now
I'll try to tell you all I have heard.  The first 'phone call was from
the Peoria Tribune office.  He said they had a letter from someone living
in Arizona wanting them to put forth an effort to locate me."
     "Why Mom, what in the world are they going to do with you?" asked
Arthur.
     "Oh, nothing at all dear.  Mother can't imagine what they want to
find her for, but we don't have to worry about their being anything dis-⟦hyphen⟧
graceful about it.  Probably some old Sunday school mate or something,"
she hoped to quiet their fears.
     "Maybe they want to kidnap her," laughed Daddy.
     "All I know to do is to wait and see," and Allie rose to clear the
table.  The boys helped her.  One after another offered suggestions to
what might be the meaning of it all.  Allie had little to offer.  She
knew as well as if she had been told by the party itself.  As the last
dishes were placed in the cupboard a sharp rap was heard on the door.
Johnie opened it.  A stranger entered.
     "I maybe taking liberties, Mr. Bradford, but I have with me a copy
of tonight's Peoria Tribune which will explain to you my visit here.   I
have come to interview your wife." He opened it wide and held it out in
front of him so they could all read the headlines.
     Allie entered the room and Johnie introduced her to the reporter.
He handed⟦strikeover: letters overtyped mid-word; reads like "hanied"⟧ the paper to her.  Her eyes fell on the bold words, "Search
for Alice Parepa-Roza Wesley".  Has any body here seen "Alice"?  The
following communication was received today by the Peoria ⟦strikeover: Evening⟧ Tribune .
