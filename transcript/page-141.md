                              CHAPTER XXVI

        That night proved to be the most restless night ever passed by
four people.  Aunt Kate was sure there was something worrying Parepa
she could hear her tossing in the bed across the room from her own.
Or was it because she was excited with the expectation of seeing her
lover.
        Parepa was concerned only about Joseph.  She was so sorry for him.
She had enjoyed his companionship and jolly good jokes a lot, now she
finds he actually loves her.  Loves her as much as she loves Frances.
        Joseph turned and tossed in fevered torment trying to be brave
and take his defeat like a man as he had met all other (and they were
many) disappointments.
        After leaving the Slay home Frances as if in a dream strolled down
to the river bank.  He was sure he recognized the very log on which he
and Parepa had sat after their wonderful boa⟦unclear: t⟧ ride on the river.  He
sat down on the log and tried to decide what was right to do.  He
found all the old love coursing through his every vein.  His body
surged and swayed as he sat there alone in the moonlight - the same
moon - the same river - Yes, the same old log - but where is the sweet
young thing he had held in his arms.  Where is the golden voice -
the sparkling eyes, the ruby lips, the tawny hair, and the kisses.  Oh
god!  the kisses.  Suddenly as if he had been committing some theft he
sprang to his feet and ran down the shore for many blocks, then into
the street where he slackened his speed until he reached the hotel.
