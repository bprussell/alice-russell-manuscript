                              CHAPTER XVI

     They were home now and found Uncle and Auntie on the porch waiting
for them.
     "Let's all go to the show Saturday night," suggested Frances.  "Dad
will like to go, too, I know".
     "It won't be a very high class show," said Parepa, "but I am crazy
to go.  Some one told me it was mostly acts put on by individuals and
not a real play."
     "Well, we'll all go anyway if you say so."  Mr. Slay said to
Frances.
     So it was settled and at eight o'clock Frances called for them.
     Parepa was not there but Uncle George explained that she would be
late in arriving at the box on account of an unexpected turn of affairs.
However, they were to leave her ticket at the box office and she would
join them when she arrived.  Mr. Wheeler was waiting in the lobby and
the four of them took their place in the box.
     The performance had began but no Parepa had joined them.  The
second act was to be a dance and song act interspersed with impersona-⟦hyphen⟧
tions.  To the surprize of the occupants  of a certain box, the actress
was unmistakably Parepa-Roza.  They sat enthralled with the rest of
the audiance.  Such a surprize!  Her dancing and acting was perfect,
her singing heavenly.  How did she dare do it .  The genius of this
young actress made a memorable picture.  The expression on the faces of
these people changed but were non-committal.  They were tense with
