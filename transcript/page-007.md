                                                                    7

Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

with the hospitable Jacksons.
     Mrs Jackson came out to the wagon for some coffee which she
declared⟦strikeover: "la" overtyped on other letters⟧ they were out of and on seeing Parepa sitting on the seat
said, "Well, where⟦strikeover: letters overtyped mid-word⟧ did you get the girl?  Did someone trade her in
on coffee and spices?"
     "No, that's my girl", bid Uncle George.
     Parepa didn't correct him for she had begun to imagine herself
as partly Uncle George's girl anyway and was so hungry for some dinner
and a rest from riding that the small matter of who she belonged to
was nothing in comparison.
     Mrs Jackson called to her seventeen year old daughter to show
Parepa where she could "wash up" and at once they were chattering away
like they had always known each other.  At the dinner table Mrs
Jackson said "I should just your girl and our Ruth are about the same
age, Ruth's sixteen."
     "Just about", said Uncle George and changed the subject. Score
one, thought Parepa-Roza.  "From now on I'm sixteen".
     The meal progressed with the usual bantering and jokes between
Uncle George and Mrs Jackson.  Parepa-Roza was quiet.  She had
"reclined within herself" as big brother use to say when she closed
her mouth and ears to those around her.  After Mrs Jackson had traded
in her "tea tickets" for a kettle and a tin cup the two mounted the
seat and were off on their last stretch homeward.
