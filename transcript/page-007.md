Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

                              Chaper II

     Neither said a word for sometime.  Uncle George⟦strikeover: letters "or" overtyped on other letters mid-word⟧ finally broke
the silence by saying, "So you are Ruth's age, hey? about sixteen".
     "Yes," spoke up Parepa Roza quickly, "and from now on don't you
ever forget it."
     "I won't,"promised Uncle George faithfully and he never did.
     At each place they stopped to sell their wares Parepa-Roza tried
not to be noticed as she didn't crave attention, but Uncle George
just couldn't keep from introducing his (sixteen year old) niece to
his customers.  They all had the usual complimentary things to say
about her but to Alice Parepa-Roza they meant nothing and it bored
her to have to listen and "smile back" to such ordinary talk.
     The only thing about Uncle George's business that really interested
Parepa-Roza was watching the customers "pick out" their premiums for
their "tea tickets".
     After they had left the last place on the route she said, "Uncle
George, do you know not one of those women took a pretty think.  They
all chose ugly old kettles, wash-pans, or mop-sticks?  Why don't you
suppose they take pretty things when they are getting them for nothing?
Just think how pretty Mrs Jackson's shelf would look with Ruth's
picture in one of those pretty celluloid frames and her dinner table
would have been so beautiful with a bouquet of those petunias in one
of these pretty vases.  I could see that third one back really wanted
that pretty matting rug for her tickets and I felt so sorry for her
when she put it back and took the homliest thing you had, that horrid
old spitoon for her husband to spit in and I'll bet she will be the
one that will have to empty it too.  Uncle George, I want you to
promise me something.  Will you?"
