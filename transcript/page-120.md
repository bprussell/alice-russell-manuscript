                              CHAPTER XXIII

     After a wonderful night's sleep and rest Frances awoke happy and
determined to make Parepa spend the happiest day of her life.  He would
not mention his story or anything that would cause a cloud to cross their
path.
     The telephone rang - and it was Parepa calling so early.  They
chatted and laughed awhile and then began to plan the day's pleasure.
     "Let's go for a canter this morning," suggested Parepa.
     "Fine!" said Frances.  "I'll be out with the span we rode the
last time.  Be there at eight o'clock."  When he turned from the 'phone
Daddy Wheeler stood facing him.
     After greetings he said, "I wonder if it wouldn't be a wise thing
for you to avoid mentioning that subject today.  It's your last day
together and why not make it an entirely happy one?  Glad you are going
for a ride this nice morning - nothing better for young folks," he added.
     Frances was sure Daddy had seen a wakeful night.  Why had he
worried him about this affair with Parepa.  At eight o8clock he and Parepa
were in the saddle making the most beautiful picture Aunt Kate and Uncle
George had ever seen.
     "They are just made for each other," Uncle George was sure of
that.
     "Parepa is far too old for her age, George.  She is as mature in
mind and body as a twenty year old.  Seems as if she never was a real
little girl.  The two years she did herself out of when she came her was
a crime.  I would not have allowed her to carry such a burden of work and
study had I any idea she was only fourteen," and Aunt Kate sat down in
an easy chair beside her husband.
