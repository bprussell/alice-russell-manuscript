     On the other side of the fence the cows and horses nibbled contented-⟦hyphen⟧
ly at the green grass.  Companionship seemed essential to their happiness.
A solitary tree, standing apart, looked sad and lonesome.  Allie fell to
thinking about the great necessity of companionship to ones happiness.
She come to realize a really good companion is a rare find.  It takes
so many ingredience to make one.  Tolerance, consideration for the
feelings of another, honesty of conduct, the ability to overlook, the
knack of understanding, the courage to be ones self, and many more
qualities to make a genuine companionship.  She looked into the face of
her friend by her side and saw there all the things envolved in a real
companion.  The lasting things, the things that count.

     He turned to her with the question, "A penny for your thoughts.
You are surely having some deep ones.  Most forgot I was here I think.
Out with them.  What were you thinking about?" and he shook her as if
trying to waken her from a dream.

     She laughed and said, "You'd laugh at me if I were to tell you.
You'd think I was silly."

     "Try me and see," he answered pulling some green grass and tossing
it in her lap.

     "Well, then, I was thinking how lonesome it would be in this world
if one had to live alone - always - like that tree over there by itself.
It actually looks sad don't it, Johnie?" and she looked up into his
eyes for his answer.
