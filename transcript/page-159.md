     It was growing dusk now and throwing her long cape over both
their shoulders, cuddling up close in his arms with her head on his
shoulder she said, "Do -- do you love me?"
     He took her chin in his hand and turning her face to his he
said, "Look in my eyes, dearest - what do you see?"
     She could not answer for they sat in a tense embrace.  Then as if
suddenly reminded of his duty he said, "Parepa ⟦strikeover: "M" overtyped on another letter⟧y mother was a brave
woman, much braver than her child.  She knew the ⟦illegible⟧⟦pencil-insert: shame⟧ her ancestors as
well as she herself had brought up on the name of Emmerson and society.
Her mother had tried to correct it and resist the temptations that lead
to disgrace.  She had not the strength of character to overcome her
weakness.  Then my mother fell in love with Daddy Wheeler - ".
     "Oh, and is he your father?" exclaimed Parepa almost happily.
     "No, I'm sorry to say he was not.  Mr. Wheeler was ten years older
than my mother and for that reason he would not confess the true love
he had for her.  As his love for her grew he decided to go to Germany
and study music.  While he was gone his father, who was my mother's
guardian, placed my mother in a finishing school in the west.
     She imagined herself in love with a dashing young fellow handsome
in face and manner.  They sinned - my mother had forgotten her vow that
she never would fall as her mother's before her had done.  Such was the
weakness of the flesh.  The sins of the parents visit upon their
children in this way.  When my mother found her weakness and realized
how impossible it was when born in the blood to overcome it she determined
to end her disgraceful line, by bringing into the world a son.  All
the time she carried me near her heart she thought of me only as her
son.  She wrote to me and talked of me to her intimates (who were few)
