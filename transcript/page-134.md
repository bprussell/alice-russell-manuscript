                              CHAPTER XXV

        Days, weeks, months and to Parepa ages went rolling away in the
two years that followed.  It was a hard two years for Parepa as Uncle
George's death left Aunt Kate in a broken condition on health.  It had
been necessary for them to see the home which they so loved in order to
keep Uncle George in the hospital and continue the services of the best
Doctors.  The moved into a little cottage of four rooms near the Bethany
Baptist church which cornered on Hayward and Madison Streets.
        Parepa became much interested in this Church and the Sunday School
work to say nothing of the young man Joseph Donald by name who taught a
class of boys just opposite her class of girls.  Oh, of course, Parepa
wouldn't have admitted she was interested in this smart industrious young
fellow and farther than their class interested threw them together but
never-the-less there was a little "hankerin" for each others company just
the same.  The young man was earning his way through Bradley college and
had won several scholarships that made him all the more determined to
succeed even though the profession he had chosen for himself would cost
him most of his time and all his earnings.
        How he wished he could afford to take Parepa out and show her a
good time - just once - but no, he needed every penny if he was to
succeed.  He had a fair voice and they both sang in the choir.  After
evening services he would stroll home with her, the half block, and then
sometimes would "come in a minute" and sometimes he would "just sit on
the porch a minute".  Parepa admired this young fellow so much.  He had
such a strong purpose in life and such a strong will to carry out that
purpose.
