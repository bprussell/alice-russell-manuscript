     Early the next morning Uncle George was "hitched up" and after

good-byes, the two of them were off for Peoria.  As far back as Parepa

"big hill" they went out of sight to the home folks.
     To them, Parepa was going on her vacation, the first one of
John Wesley's children to ever be away from home without their folks;
to Parepa she was going for a job.  It had been in her mind for weeks
but just how she was going to manage the "get away" had been her
only worry.  Now it had come about in such a natural way that Parepa
pinched herself to be sure it was really she that was sitting upright
on the hard rough seat of the covered tea wagon.
     Uncle George tried to visit with her to keep her from getting
lonesome.  She was quiet but he was mistaken if he thought for one
minute she was lonesome.  She was real busy trying to plan the best
way to broach the subject that was upper most in her mind to Uncle
George.  Finally he made it easy for her by saying, "Do you know
Parepa-Roza, your Auntie and I would just like to have a little girl
like you to live with us all the time.  Don't see why John needs so
many kids and I just think we'll keep you in Peoria."  Much to his
surprize she calpped her hands and said, "Oh, Uncle George, would
you really and truly keep me?  I'll be so good if you only will."
