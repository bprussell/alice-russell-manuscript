                              CHAPTER VII

     They were all ready now and at promptly five-thirty a carriage
from the best liverys⟦pencil-delete⟧ in town drove up in front of the house.  Frances
stepped out and ran up the steps to meet them.  The driver stepped
down from his high seat and helped the four of them in.  Frances and
Parepa sat facing Uncle George and Aunt Kate.  They joked and laughed
all the way.  Parepa told Frances she was to stay right on with Aunt
Kate and he clapped his hands delightedly.  Then his face clouded and
 he said soberly, "Wish I could stay right on, too."
     "Oh, so do I," said Parepa truthfully.  "Don't you suppose that
man,  ---   your guarantee or what ever you call him will let you if
we all ask him?"
     "You mean his guardian," correct Aunt Kate.
     "I'm afraid not," and he sobered thoughtfully.  Then, as if he
must not forget he was responsible for the merriment of his guests he
roused himself quickly and began telling a funny story.  They all
laughed and then Uncle George told one.  By that time they were nearing
the Hotel.  The driver drew up on the reins and stepped down from his
seat, opened the door and helped them to the street.  Parepa was so
amazed at the splendor before her.
     "My, this must of cost an awful lot," she whispered to Uncle George
and her sparkling eyes drank in the beauty around her.  She scarcely
knew what they were saying as she sat entranced, glancing shyly at
the beautiful masterly young boy that was seated by her side.  "He seems
to be used to all this" she thought.  "He doesn't even notice all the
pretty things around us."
     They were laughing and joking now but Parepa was afraid this dream
would fade away and she would find herself back home eating home cured
