     "That will be great fune," he assured her and the time was set
for six o'lock.
     "We'll ride up the river road to Mossville and take breakfast at
Crawl's Inn," he suggested.  "Daddy Wheeler and I have made it several
times this summer.  It's just enough sprint to give a fellow a good
appetite."
     At five-thirty the next morning Parepa doned her new bicycle
costume,⟦strikeover: heavy comma, possibly struck over "s"⟧ which Aunt Kate had made of the prettiest dark red serge.

little cap to match pulled over her tawny head, jerked a little to one
side, seemed to invite the approval of disapproval of those "privileged"
(as Aunt Kate considered it) to gaze on her.
     Most girl bicylists wore blue or grey outfits and Parepa felt
quite "set up2⟦strikeover: "2" struck over the closing quotation mark⟧ in this attractive different suit.  She was all animation

Aunt Kate glowing with pride waved them a long farewell.
     Frances wore cycling suit of gray check knickers, white shirt, and
an unmistakably red tie that floated over his shoulder in the breeze.
His skull cap fitted tightly to his head.  They were both good cyclists.
Parepa had learned on Aunt Kate's old one before they had traded it in
on a tandam which she and Uncle George enjoyed together.
     The morning air, and exercise put color in these children's cheeks
and sparkle in their eyes.  As they sat at the little breakfast table
each thought the other the most beautiful person in the world.  After
eating they strolled to the river and stood whispering little nothings
in each others ears until time to start home.
