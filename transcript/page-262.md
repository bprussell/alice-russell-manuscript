Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

                         CHAPTER XXIV

     "Oh, that  was easy," the old fellow chuckled.  "When Frances found
out your present name she at once wrote checks to me for the amounts
and hurried me off to Pheonix with two of them, eachon different banks
there, and to Tucson banks with the other two.  You will have to sign
the signature cards and I'll send them in to the banks today.  I
deposited two hundred and fifty thousand dollars in each of the four
banks for you as a checking account.  You can do as you please with it.
Leave it in Arizona banks or change it to your home banks.  My advise
to you in these days of bank failures all over the United States is,
never keep a great amount in any one bank and never let one bank know
how much you have in another bank.  Always be fair with the government.
Keep your business affairs as simple as possible.  Avoid complications.
As soon as you return home employ the very best Secretary you can find
to take care of your affairs.  Never turn your business over to anyone,
I mean by that, keep your authority and don't allow yourself to be
talked out of or into anything.  Use your own best judgment alwyas but
be sure it <u>is</u> your best.  Be fair and true to yourself and others and
most of all to your God.  Be not over anxious for this world's goods.
Be satisfied with fair returns.  Never plunge or speculate.  Always
remember we are all 'Tenants of the Lord' - Care-takers for him.  These
are the ⟦unclear: princepals⟧ on which the Emmerson fortune has always been handled.
You can never go wrong by following them.

     Here, take care of these, they belong to you," he said handing her
the deposit books and checks.  "Both Frances and I feel sure she could
not have trusted her fortune to better hands."

     "Oh, thank you Daddy," she said.  "I know you shall not be dis-⟦hyphen⟧
appointed in me.  It's hard for me to believe its true."
