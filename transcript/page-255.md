                         CHAPTER XXIII

     Parepa was shocked at first to see the form of her ardent  lover
of the past clad in richest feminine garb.  Frances held out her arms
to her.  Parepa fell to her knees beside her and for a long time neither
spoke as they held cheek to cheek in fondest embrace.

     "So good of your to come, dearest," Frances broke the spell.

     "I wanted to come." she said patting her hand.

     "You have made me better already.  I have gained ever since we got
your message.  I'm so glad we found you."

     Parepa rose and Daddy placed a chair for her.

     "We got thirty-two telegrams and letters in answer to our letter
to the paper," she said and added, "I think we know all there is to
know about you now."

     "Then I won't have to tell you," she laughed.

     "It seems good to see you looking so well, Parepa.  I'm so glad
you are happily married and have a family.  It was best, wasn't it
dearest?" and Frances smiled up into her face that old smile that Parepa
never could forget.

     "You haven't changed much Frances," she said encourageingly.  "I
expected to see a real invalid."

     "It's my heart," she said.  "I have to be careful."  Just then a
nurse came into the room.  Frances introduced them and added, "This is
my best girlhood chum.  She has come from Illinois to see me."

     "That's fine.  Now Miss Emmerson I'm afraid you will have to
excuse yourself and take some rest."  Turning to Parepa she added, "I'm
sorry, Mrs Bradford, but Doctor's orders are orders you know."

     "I'll see you after dinner, Parepa," and Frances was rolled through
big doors into her sleeping room.
