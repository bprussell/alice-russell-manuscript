                          CHAPTER XX

     One evening before sunset Frances rented a small row boat at
the foot of Hayward Street and they rowed out to the middle of the
river.  There they lifted the oars and drifted - drifted - drifted
where they may, for several hours.  The round red sun sank behind the
sky line and the moon rose over the eastern horizon full and beautiful.
The stars shone like diamonds.  The city's lights danced and sparkled
like so many jewels set in black onyx.  Once in a while the shrill
whistle of a locomotive would cut through the silence which made this
evening and location so irresistible to these two young lovers.  For
lovers they were, and neither tried to argue themselves out of it.
     Parepa lay limp and trusting in the arms of the one she loved.
She was happy - Oh, so happy!  Frances was all in the world she wanted
and the bliss that was hers as she gave herself up completely to his
ardent kisses and carresses was the bliss of a clean young love⟦unclear: r⟧.
     "Parepa, darling, you are all in this world I have to love.  I
love you so much I am a coward, a coward about telling you some things
you must know before I dare to ask you to be mine forever.  Forgive
me dearest for never having told you before.  I have wanted to, but
I couldn't bring myself to do it."  He held her closer kissing her lips,
her hair, her eye lids passiomately.  "Can you listen to my story now
dear?"
     "Oh Frances, Please.  I never want to listen to it.  I never want
to know.  All I want is all your love, all of you.  I care nothing for
your story," and her eyes turned to his, pleaded for her.
     "You will have to know someday, dearest.  I am not a brute, and
I will not take your innocent love unless you will listen to my story
from beginning to end.  Are you⟦strikeover: overtyped character⟧ ready?"
