                              CHAPTER V

     Parepa could hear a low argument going on outside but could not
see the parties taking part in it.  However, she was soon to be in-⟦hyphen⟧
troduced to the main actor.

     Harold broke past the boy who was trying to persuade him to go
to room and sober up before letting Parepa see him.  "She's my girl
and I'll see her if I want too," he stammered in a voice not at all
familiar to Parepa.

     Before she could turn to see him he fell over her chair with arms
around her and kissing her cheek he said, "Dish is my sweetheart and
nobody can stop me from seeing her.  Yer glad I come ain't you, honey?
Ain't you now?" and he gave an ugly lunge over her shoulder again,
"Give me a sweet kish, honey."

     Parepa beat him off and all the boys at the table rose to her
defense and drug him from the house and down the street.  He was angry
drunk.  The first person Parepa had ever seen in that condition.

     "What is the matter with him, is he sick?" she asked in blank
surprize.

     "Yes, drunk sick," said the old maid teacher who sat at the head
of the table.  "We all wanted to tell you but none of us had the nerve.
It isn't his first offense by any means."

     "You mean  -  you mean he gets drunk?" and she fell forward.  She
had fainted.

     The girls all rushed to help her.  They laid her on Mrs Allen's
bed and called a doctor.  "It was a terrible shock to the child," Mrs
Allen told him.  "Harold has told the boys they are to be married at
the end of the term."
