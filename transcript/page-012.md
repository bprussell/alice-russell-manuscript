     "I8d love to" ventured our heroine and as she passed the long
mirror in the hall she hardly knew herself.  "I really an pretty",
she said to herself, "Funny I never noticed it before."
     "Guess I'll let you fix the salad", said Aunt Kate, smiling.
     "You'll have to show me how but I'll like doing it."  She
remembered seeing a salad at the Church supper once and it looked
pretty but she didn't risk eating it for fear she wouldn't handle it
just right.  Now she was actually going to fix one.
     "All right," said Aunt Kate, "we'll make the one your Uncle
George likes best.  It won't take long.  First, you can cut up these
two apples into small pieces, wash them first; then cut this celery
in just as small pieces.  You see I've had it washed and in this
clean bag on the ice so it will be nice and crisp.  Open this little
can of tuna fish, salt, and mix them all together by putting them into
this white bowl and tossing about with this wooden fork.  Then mix in
this salad dressing.  After that is done you can put a lettuce leaf on
each salad plate and put some salad on the leaf.  I always put some
of the dressing on the table in this mayonnaise bowl so if anyone
cares for more they can help themselves."
     Through all this long speech Parepa-Roza stood spell bound.
Such a pretty kitchen, such beautiful dishes!  She didn't think she
would ever get tired washing them.  If mamma only had pretty things.
And the curtains!  Think of it, thin white curtains trimed with a
blue border - in the <u>kitchen</u>, mind you.
