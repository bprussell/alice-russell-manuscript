                              CHAPTER XIII

     In these two years letters came from and went to Frances at
regular intervals.  Parepa always read them and deposited them in her
"keep sake drawer" as she called it, never once offering them to Aunt
Kate to read.  Mrs Slay was amused by this little trick in her neice,
that was so unlike her, but was satisfied to hear only what Parepa
was witting to diverge.
     She knew from the size of the incoming and outgoing letters that
there couldn't have been much-left unsaid.  Parepa would always say,
"He sends you both his love," and Uncle George would tease, "And what
did he send you."  A witty answer was always on her tongue end.
     One June day the postmakr was unmistakably Halifax, Nova Scotia.
     "He's getting nearer, I see," said Uncle George as he handed her
the letter.
     "Oh, goody, goody, aren't we glad?"
     "Bet we are," said Aunt Kate, "unless he has changed a lot."
     "Oh, he hasn't one bit I know," and Parepa disappeared into her
bedroom as was her custom when she had an unopened letter in her hand.
     Uncle George and Aunt Kate fell to reviewing the changes Frances
might see in Parepa.
     "She is much more beautiful, taller, more stately, can beat him
I'm sure in tennis, can swim, a splendid dancer, is beautiful in the
saddle, and can enjoy all the auto rides he's able to furnish.  Won't
he be surprized to find her soloist in the church he attended the last
Sunday he was here and giving whole evening dramatic entertainments?
Also to find her still working in the "candy shop"." said Uncle
George.
