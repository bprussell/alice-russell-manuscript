                              CHAPTER XIX

     The 'phone rang, Robert answered it.  "It's long distance, Mother,
they want to talk to you."
     Allie took the receiver shakingly in her hand.
     "Hello," she said.
     "This is a reporter for the Peoria Tribune speaking Mrs Bradford.
Were you,before your marriage, Miss Alice Parepa-Roza Wesley?" the
voice inquired.
     "I was," she answered calmly.
     "And did you ever reside with relatives in the neighborhood of the
Bethany Baptist Church in Peoria?" he quized.
     "I did," was her answer.
     "I want to advise you Mrs Bradford that we received a communication
from a party in Arizona whose name I will withhold for the present, asking
us to make an effort to locate⟦strikeover: "o"/"c" double-struck⟧ you."
     "Thank you very much," was her only reply.
     "This person, it seems, is in very poor health and is desirous of
locating you immediately."  A pause followed.  "Have you a message?"
he a sked.
     "None, I thank you."
     "We published this communication in this evening's paper, hoping to
hear from someone who knew of your where-abouts, and have been kept busy
answering 'phone calls from friends of your every since."  He waited for
a reply.  There was none and he continued.  "I'd advise you to get in
touch with him as soon as possible.  It may be to your advantage."
     "Thank you very much," she said as if to close the conversation.
     "Would you like to know the signature to the message?"
     "You may tell me if you care too," she said.
