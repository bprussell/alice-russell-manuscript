     "Let's go tomorrow evening then", said Frances not willing to
drop the subject.
     "Yes," said Aunt Kate, "we'll take a picnic lunch and eat out
there.  Would you like that Parepa?"
     "Oh, yes, I've always wished I could see a picnic --- or be at
one".  She hesitated, knowing she had said the wrong thing.  Her cheeks
turned a deep crimson and she could feel herself slipping.  The rest
of them kept up a round of chatter but our heroine didn't risk another
sentence during the meal.  She was actually sweating now and Uncle
George knew she was laboring harder to get done with that meal than
she ever did milking the five cows which was her usual allotment each
morning and evening.
     She will never know how she got through that meal.  It was all
a blur to her, but when Aunt Kate said as they rose from the table,
"Parepa-Roza and I will do up the dishes and then we'll be out on the
porch with you", she thought that for a few moments at least she would
be away from the eyes and ears of this strange boy.  No such luck.
"Let me help you Mrs Slay?  I can wash dishes as well as a woman so
let me prove it?" said the unsuspecting Frances as he gathered his
hands full of dirty dishes.  "Right this way my ladies" and he bowed
a low bow into the kitchen.
