Cities of every State in the Union and in some of the other large

put too many eggs in one basket you know," he chuckled as he saw
Parepa's eyes open when large amounts were mentioned.

     "You don't mean that Frances has that much money," she gasped.

     "Yes, and lots left for herself, should she need it.  I am to dis-⟦hyphen⟧
pose of it to Charities after she is through with it.  Come, Now, its
most dinner time," and they strolled into the house.

     Parepa and Daddy ate together in the beautifully appointed dining
room, with all the service accompanying a formal dinner.  Parepa was
very tired.  She hoped she could gracefully retire early.

     This wish was quite naturally granted as Frances was only allowed
to visit one half hour after dinner.  She went to her sitting room and
took from the writing desk daintily perfumed stationary.  She wrote a
long, long, letter to her loved ones at home telling them of the
beauty of the country, of Frances's condition and assured them of her
own good health.  After she had sealed the envelope her maid sent it
to the Post Office with a servant.

     There was lots of good reading on her table but she was so tired.
"I believe I'll retire now," she told her maid."  After a shower and
brisk rub she slipped into her night gown and after her prayers were
said got into the most luxurious bed she had ever seen.

     "I'll be in this room, Mrs Bradford," said her maid, " should you
need me just push this button.  Good night."

     "Good night dear," and Parepa sank into a restful slumber.  She
did not waken through the night.  It was nine o'clock the next morning
before she dressed.  Her breakfast was brought to her sitting room.
She relished  every morsel of it.
