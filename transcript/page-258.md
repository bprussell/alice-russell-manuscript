     At ten o'clock she decended the stairs and found Daddy waiting for
her in the library.  After cheerful greetings were exchanged between
them two nurses entered pushing the couch on which lay Frances.  Beautiful
as a flower, but as fragil.  Her hair was as soft and wavy as when Parepa
knew her and not one gray hair could be seen.  The nurses left them
alone.

     "Oh, how beautiful you are Frances!" exclaimed Parepa.  "I know
you must feel fine today," and she stooped and kissed her.

     "I do, thanks to you Parepa dear," she answered.  "I'm all ready
to hear all about my old sweetheart."

     They all three laughed heartily over the joke.  It really seemed to
them now as if it had been one of fate's huge jokes.

     "I want to hear your whole story since we parted.  I see many
interesting tell tale impressions which time has left on your counten-⟦hyphen⟧
ance.  Has the world dealt a little hard with you sometimes, Parepa?"
she asked.

     "I wouldn't say hard, exactly," she answered.  "But I have seen a
great deal of joy and trouble in the last thirty years.  Had I not had
the companionship of the kindest husband in the world I never could have
survived it.  You can see we were not blessed with this worlds goods.
In fact there were times when we hardly knew where the next dollar was
coming from.  Yet, I have been a fortunate mother and wife and I'm not
complaining."
