        Again they sat silently basking in their love for one another.
Finally Frances said,  'What are your plans for study these next two years?"
        "I haven't any," she said frankly.  "I am going to spend  my time
helping Uncle George and Aunt Kate.  You know I couldn't be an expense
to them now that he has had to give up work.  Mrs Breen has great plans
for she and I to go about giving recitals over the nearby country.  She
is booking dates for us now.  She will be my accompanist when I sing and
we will both give readings and short sketches for two.  Won't it be fun?
I can still keep my job at the candy store."
        Again they sat silent Parepa picturing herself on the platform
of small town Churches or halls while Frances was trying to plan out a
way of leaving a bank account for this independent young thing that she
might keep on with her music lessons at least.  It was growing dusk now
and Parepa sprang from her seat with an "Up! Up! my lad!  'Tis time to
flee."
        They walked home hand in hand.  When they reached the house Parepa
recognized the neighborhood Doctor hurrying into their house.
        "What can be the matter?  Someone is sick,' and Parepa broke into
a run.  As they entered they saw Aunt Kate tiptoe into the bedroom with
an anxious look on her face. Parepa followed.  The Doctor was bending over
Uncle George who was breathing heavily.
        "Just a slight stroke," he told Aunt Kate.  "He will come out of
it all right."
