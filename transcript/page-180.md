     What did she know about Harold's past?  Nothing - but, of course,
he is good too - she'd not doubt.that.

     Finally Johnie asked, "Have you been thinking real hard about what
we were talking about the last time we were together Allie," and he
gave her a hug and squeezed her hand lovingly.

     Parepa wished he would grab her and run far far away with her.
Why, oh, Why was he so slow?  She found hers⟦strikeover: illegible⟧f on fire at his slightest
touch.  Why couldn't he see!  Why couldn't he feel, and she turned her
head from him when she really wanted to throw herself into his arms
and cry take me!  take!  and keep me.  He was so dear, so disturbing.

     "No, Johnnie, I haven't thought anything about it.  You know I'm
going to teach another year.  There will be time enough later." she
said calmly (good acting)⟦pencil-delete⟧

     She could feel his arm loosening, his hand clasp weakening.  His
heart sink.  "Maybe I shouldn't have come?" he said, "maybe you would
have had a better time with some one else.  Tell me Allie shall I go
home in the morning?  I don't want to spoil your good times: really I
don't.  I was foolish enough to think maybe you would have some good
news to tell me.  I guess I'm too anxious.  I really don't want to hurry
you.  I do so miss you, Allie, you don't know how I've missed  you,"
and he rose from the seat and helped her to her feet.

     "I'm really glad you came, Johnie," she said.  "I would have been
lonesome too if you hadn't come.  Tomorrow we will have another god
visit."

     They walked back to the Hotel chatting together about this one and
that one at home and parted wishing each other a good night.
