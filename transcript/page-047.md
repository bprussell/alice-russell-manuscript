sleep when the horses stopped at the house.
     Frances whispered to Aunt Kate "he'd go on over home and not stop
tonight.  I'll run over early in the morning and see how she is," he
said as he left them.
     After they were in the house Parepa looked at her tear stained dress
and soaked handkerchief and cried again.
     "There, there, sweetheart, don't cry anymore  --  Aunt Kate will
help you to bed and tomorrow you will be a new girl.  Good night," and
he gave her a fatherly kiss.
     "Good night, Uncle George, and I'm so sorry I spoiled your evening.
I'll try ever so hard never to cry again."  Aunt Kate was leading her
up the steps now and soon she would be in her bed alone.  Oh, happy
thought!  She soon sobbed herself to sleep.  In the morning she wakened
as fresh and sweet as a rose.  Aunt Kate helped her into her pretty new
shop dress and neither referred to the experiences of the evening be-⟦hyphen⟧
fore.
     At the breakfast table Uncle George kept them laughing at his
jokes.
     Parepa practiced as she never did before.  Aunt Kate and Uncle
George listened intently as they pretended to be clearing the dishes.
     She is simply a wonder", said Aunt Kate.  "We are going to have
something to be mighty proud of I'll tell you.  Her voice is the sweetest
and fullest I ever heard in one so young.  Oh, I'm so happy George, <u>So</u>
happy!
