                              CHAPTER XVII

     They had many orders for their dahlia bulbs and now the time had
come to open the bulb pit and fill these orders.  Their dismay can be
imagined when the⟦pencil-insert: y⟧ found every bulb was rotted.  They viewed the desolation
with tears in their eyes.  Allie was so overcome by this loss and dis-⟦hyphen⟧
appointment that it was necessary for her to take her bed for a few days.
The first time in her life she gave herself up to her feelings.  Her
courage was completely downed.  She had met adversities bravely time
after time.  Now she was unable to overcome this great shock.  Her fires
of hope for this new year were burned away leaving only their gray
scattered ashes.  It wasn't like Allie to give up this way.  It worried
Johnie who depended so much on her strength and encouragement in times
like this.
     After all did she not have a right to this state of mind?  Why
shouldn't she indulge in the resentment she felt against the unfair
dealings of fate.  Slowly she recovered her strength and courage and once
again was ready to carry on just the same, start all over again and
trust in God.  She did not believe that"standing still and waiting" would
bring back prosperity to them.
     These simple people lived on faith.  They liked the Bible definition
of faith "the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not
seen."  For a few days only their faith was wounded.  At heart they
were optimists whose sole food was faith.  Faith in the God of the
birds and of the flowers.  Thankful were they always to this God for the
free wealth of the beauties of this world that no billionaire even
could buy and hid from them.  They were ever humbly heroic.  When they
were troubled or discouraged they thought of how much worse things
might be and thanked their God for the many blessing received.
