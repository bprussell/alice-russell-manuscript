Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

The mails were full of catalogs from every aviation school he could
locate through advertisements.  Some of the money he earned by helping
neighbors with their work went for subscriptions to the Aerial Digest
and other periodicals on the subject.

     Richard at seventeen vowed his allegiance to the farm.  He loved
the stock, the fields, the whole out doors.  Base ball, basket ball,
and volley ball were his favorite sports.  The Bradford home was always
the meeting place for the young fellows of the neighborhood.  John⟦strikeover: "n" double-struck⟧nie
and Allie encouraged the boys in having young company at their home
but not during working hours.  They were taught to "Work while you
work, Play while you play.  That is the way to be. - Cheerful and Gay."

     Arthur was fifteen now and as changeable in his ideas as he was in
his moods.  He early dec⟦strikeover: "l" double-struck⟧lared his dislike for the opposite sex and made
his "get away" as soon as he sensed the approach of such.  He was in
his first year high school now and doing nicely.  It was an effort to
raise the five dollars per week board this winter as a good many of the
villagers that bought milk from the Bradfords were out of work and  could
not pay for it.  They kept a tin box on the side board in which they put
the money for this purpose.  Some times they would lack some and would
have to take a chicken or two to town to make up the lacking board
money.  All this they kept from Arthur knowing it would worry him.

     The fall and winter passed.  Spring stood upon the threshhold begging
admittance.
