Edelstein, Illinois
                              CHAPTER IX

     "Won't we all be lonesome though, when he goes home," said
Parepa wistfully.
     "We will miss him but you will get acquainted with others as you
go out more," comforted Uncle George.
     "Yes, but I'll never feel so acquainted with anyone as I do with
him.  Seems most as if our dog had died or something.  Anyway I'm going
to cry," and she did.  Cried as though her heart would break.
     "There, there," said Aunt Kate," You are homesick, that's all.
Frances has been company for you and you haven't missed your brothers
and sisters.  Come now, let's go in and have a lunch."
     "Aunt K-K-Kate," stammered Parepa, "Who's that", and she pointed
to a stately important looking man stepping from a carriage in front
of Frances's boarding place.
     "I wonder if that isn't Frances's guardian," volunteered Uncle
George.
     "Come, let's all go in now," urged Aunt Kate.  They went in and
while Parepa and Aunt Kate set out a lunch Uncle George posted himself
by the parlor window to watch proceedings across the street.  He didn't
see a great deal.  Frances greeted the rather young looking middle
aged man with the warmest welcome and the two disappeared into the
house.  Both seemed overjoyed to see each other.
     "I supposed his guardian would be a real old man, but this fellow
is a man in the prime of life, younger than me I should judge,"
 announced Uncle George.
     Parepa couldn't eat.  Neither Mr. or Mrs. Slay appeared to notice
this - but they did.
     "I believe I'll go right to bed," said Parepa.
