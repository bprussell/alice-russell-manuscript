                              CHAPTER XIX

     "First, have you noticed any change in Uncle George lately?"  I
mean have you noticed him coughing?"
     "No!" exlaimed the surprized girl.  She had not expected Uncle
George's health to be the important subject of this confidential talk.
She recovered her surprize and said, "Is he really coughing much Aunt
Kate?"
     "Yes, dear, he is.  He has tried to hide it.  He has been doctoring
for several months and now the Doctor thinks it advisable for him to
give up his work and devote all his time to getting well.  It may be
advisable  for him to change climate."
     "Why Aunt Kate!  I never dreamed of such a think.  How could I
have been so blind!" she cried..
     "He has been very careful that you should not suspect.  This will
be his last trip in the tea wagon."
     Parepa felt herself a heartless creature never to have noticed when
her benefactors were in distress.
     "He didn't want one little worry to interfere with your study.  He
loves you Parepa - loves you as he would his own child, and so do I."
     Parepa threw her arms around Aunt Kate and burst into a torrant of
tears.  "I've been so ungratful!" she cried.  "I'll not take another
bit of help from you again.  From this on I am going to be the wage
earner in this family and you and Uncle George are going to have a long
rest and get well."
     "There, there, child I'm afraid I have frightened you.  Al he needs
is time to cure him.  You will be home all through the month of August
and I have been wondering if we couldn't take a  trip somewhere." Aunt
Kate said, "in celebration of your eighteenth birthday."
