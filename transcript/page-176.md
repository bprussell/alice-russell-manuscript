Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

Frances, Joseph, John⟦pencil-insert: n⟧ie, and now Harold?  Was it right to do this ⟦pencil-insert: --⟧
only to leave them - disappear from their lives altogether?  None of
them reminded her in the least of the other.  They parted that night
promising to be true to each other.

     After Parepa went to bed she felt the pangs of love twitching at
her heart strings.  Couldn't it be she was truly in love again.  Yes,
she knew she was.

     The next day found Parepa ill prepared for her classes.  The first
time in her life she had failed.  She knew the reason.  Her heart and
mind was too full of Harold.  She could think of nothing else.  It was
no use.  She <u>was</u> in love.  Head over heels in love again.  The day was
long.  When evening came two lovers were hunting a secluded place to
make plans for their future.

     "I will get a salary that will support us handsomely," he said.
"And you my lady shall be the wife of Professor H. A. Crisp.  How do
you like that, Mrs Crisp?" he laughed squeezing her hand.

     "I think that will be real brittle," she answered.  They both
laughed and then grew serious.

     "Of course, I can't buy a home yet or have everything I'd like to
have for my wife, but that will all come later.  We will love each
other so much we won't miss the things we don't have, won't we darling?"
and he kissed her again and again.

     "Indeed we will," agreed Parepa.  "I know we will be so happy."

     It was settled then and there they would be married in September.
Parepa would send her resignation to the directors of the Kellogg
school and write John⟦pencil-insert: n⟧ie a kind note telling him of her change of
heart -- or, could she?  Well, she'd see -- may be some time soon.
Not yet though; no use to hurt him yet - she'd wait until nearer time.
