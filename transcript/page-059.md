                              CHAPTER X

     "You know the man that asked me to sing in the Junior Choir
Sunday?  Well, he came into the store and said he would send his son
for me Thursday evening," said Parepa.
     "That was kind of him I'm sure" Aunt Kate answered.  "I think I'll
go over too.  I'm real interested in getting you started right.  You
have the making of a beautiful⟦strikeover: "b" overtyped on another letter⟧ contralto voice and I'm going to see
that you are placed where it will receive the most benefit."
     "I'm glad you are going.  I was dreading it a little," and Parepa
leaned over and placed a loud smack on Aunt Kate's cheek.
     They did up the few dishes and Aunt Kate suggested they sing
awhile.
     "Let's sing some of the songs I used to sing in the timber when I
when⟦strikeover: final letter overtyped⟧ for the cows", said Parepa.  "I liked to hear my echo."
     "What were they?" quized Aunt Kate.
     "Oh, 'My County 'Tis of Thee⟦strikeover: letter overtyped within word⟧', 'Almost Persuaded', Star Spangled
Banner', and I used to like to sing best of all 'In the Green Pastures
and by the Still Waters'."
     "All right that will be heaps of fun⟦strikeover: letter struck out after "fun"⟧.  You just imagine you are
down in the field now."
     "And bare-footed⟦strikeover: letter overtyped within word⟧," laughted the young girl.  "I use to sing "Pull
for the Shore' when I waded through the creeks.  The last one I learned
was 'Just One Girl'".
     Aunt Kate took her place at the piano and they sang one song after
another until both their throats were tired.
     "That's the most fun⟦strikeover: letter struck out after "fun"⟧ I've had in a long time," said Aunt Kate.
"Wish Uncle George could have heard us.  Parepa, do you know you are
going to be a great singer someday?"
