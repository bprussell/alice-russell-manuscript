imagined herself a bird, maybe an eagle, or maybe an oriole.  Any
way she was <u>always</u>something that she wasn't.  "Oh, if I were only a
boy.  God made a great mistake", she would say.  Another time it was
the big swing made of hay rope that hung from the tops of two maples.
If one dared try they could swing so high they could see clear over
the "big hill".  "One" did try real often when the folks had gone to
town.  Though the rule was no one was to swing poas ⟦pencil: past⟧ the fence.
     Then that old trunk in the attic that Dad had before he was
married.  What a store of things there was in that to be investigated.
Parepa knew some of them:  the hard-tack like Papa had to eat when
in the Civil War, one of Grandma's dresses with fringe on it, and old
bracelet, some red and silver glass vases, Mamma's school children
had given her, and Oh, Papa's and Mamma's love letters, and so many
things that she had seen but never really <u>felt</u> with her own hands.
She knew the key was kept in the old Seth Thomas Clock which stood
on the shelf in the dining room, where no one was supposed to even
look for anything.
     She never took anyone into her confidence.  Always choosing to
be alone she was exceptionally fortunate in not being caught for she
planned well and took no risks.
     She had few real chums at school for she held strictly to the
rule of "Friendly with all, but familiar with few".  Everybody liked
her and would gladly have been a pal, if not her slave, had they
gotten any encouragement.  She lived a distance of a mile from school
