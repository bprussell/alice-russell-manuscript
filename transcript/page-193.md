     If they had dared to believe store keeping was going to be an
easy job they were soon disillusioned.  They weree keeping sacred a
secret these days.  Whenever an apportunity presented itself they
would stand close together behind the counter and whisper about it.
"I hope it's a girl," was Johnie's firm statement, "Just like it's
mother."  And then there was a kiss and a hug before a customer would
enter.

     Allie didn't care what it's sex just so it was alright.  Her
whole mind was on this live thing near her heart.  It was nearly five
years since they were married.  Happy, trying years.  Years of
sacrifice and suffering.

     There had been nothing with which to satisfy the old Parepa's
love for beautiful.  No time or money for pretty gardens, dainty fancy
work, beautiful literature, or even the time to enjoy the sunsets, or
carming moonlight which always held her spell bound.  Never-the-less
she had not lost her love for beautiful things.  Now and then she caught
herself gazing into the bygone past.  She could see the gardens, the
lake, the beautiful mansion amoung the trees.  The wide walks, the
stables, with their fine steeds.  But never did she see a husband, never
did she warm to the approach of one man in these dreams.  Never did she
see her children playing in the nursery.

     Frances had said, "Some day you will be a real mother, a proud
one.  I hope there will be lots of boys."

     Indeed she was proud.  So was Johnie.
