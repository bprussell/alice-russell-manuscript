     "Do you really believe that, Johnie?" she asked.

     "Yes, I believe it as  truly as I believe anything.  Only those with
out hope need be lonesome and everyone should have hope.  If they haven't
they are 'hopeless' - entirely hopeless.  You get what I mean," he
asked.

     "I - I guess so," she answered.  She realized she lacked one thing
in her make up which she would give anything to acquire.  That thing was
understanding.  Their conversation fell in less formal channels.  All
the old crowd was discussed.  This one was going with that one, so and
so had a new buggie.  One old neighbor had died and his will was dis-⟦hyphen⟧
cussed as well as what his children would do with what they got.

     No one was forgotten.  Allie asked about them all.  They laughed
and talked until most dark.

     "Was there anything Allie would like to do tonight - any place
she would like to go in particular?"

     "No, I just like to visit," she told him as they strolled toward
the car line.  There had been no ardent love making, no pushing his
suit, or reference made to their engagement.

     It was a most restful, pleasant and enjoyable afternoon.  Such
was the result of a perfect companionship with a master mind - an
understanding mind.  No a trained or highly educated mind, not the
"master mind" one reads about in the 'ologies, but one controlled by
the natural mastery of events as they shaped his character.
