                              CHAPTER VI

     August 28th., 1898, at eight o'clock Alice Prepa-Roza Wesley was
actually launched on her industrial career in a big city.  She was so
shy, so quiet, so charmingly sweet and attentive to her duties (which
were few) that she won the love and respect of all the other employes
of the shop.
     Carry Weber and Mattie Swanson were the "old girls" on the job and
from them Parepa was to learn all the "in's and out's" of the Candy
shop business.
     "Guess we'll clean the show cases and windows first", said Mattie.
"Parepa, you can remove all the candy dishes, empty them and wash them
while we clean the glass and put new papers on the bottoms of the
showcases and window shelves."  She showed Parepa very kindly just where
to put the candy removed from dishes and how to clean and dry them.
Parepa wondered if all her work would be so easy.  Occasionally a cus-⟦hyphen⟧
tomer sauntered in and one of the older girls would wait on them.
"Some day I'll sell candy I suppose" she said to herself.  "I wonder
how long before they'll let me."
     "Say, who's the new "candy kid"? said a young swain who had been
chatting with Carry as she boxed a pound of chocolates for him.
     "Hello there, Pretty, where did you come from?" he said speaking
unmistakebly to Parepa.
     "That'll do, that'll do," said Uncle Will who was sitting quietly
in his Office cage, heretofore unnoticed.  "My helpers shall not be
addressed in so rude a manner" he said coming forward and the young
rascal took his package and went sneaking out of the door.  Parepa-Roza
blushed a deep red much to the amusement of the other girls.
