     "Yes, dear, and Uncle George and I have the picnic lunch all packed.
I'm glad you had such a fine rest.  You'll beat us all at tennis for
that, I suppose,"  she said. " Now come into the bath room and take a

Parepa?" Aunt Kate asked just to see if the girl had any idea of the
property⟦pencil: propriety⟧ of dress.
     "I'd like to wear the blue one and the tam o'shanter if you will
let me." she said to Aunt Kate's surprize.  She thought the pink or
white would probably be the silly choice of this young inexperienced
country girl.
     "That's just exactly what I hoped you'd choose." she answered.
"And the blue ribbon to match."
     It was a proud pair that decended the stairs some minutes later.
     "You better sit on the porch with me and read a little till Frances
comes over, Parepa, and then we'll go." said Uncle George wishing lots
of people would pass and gaze on their handiwork of the day.
     She placed herself proudly, book in hand, in a large chair on the
porch but if one had observed her closely they would have noticed that
her eyes cast toward the big house on the corner across the street.
She knew she was most as "pretty" as any of the children her age that
passed the house and she was really anxious for Frances to see her
before she got "mussed up".  She twisted her handkerchief in her fingers
thoughtlessly.  Finally she saw him coming.  What was that he had in
his hand?  He had on a blue suit, too, and a cap something like hers!
     "Hello, Mr. Slay, Hello Parepa-Roza.  Gee! Whiz! but your pretty."
