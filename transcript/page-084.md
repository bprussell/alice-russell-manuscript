     "Oh, Uncle Will," laughed Parepa, "you are borrowing trouble now.
Frances and I are the best of friends but that is all.  I like him
immensely and I hope he likes me as well, but neither of us are simple

as well as I think I do.  You couldn't help liking him."
     "I hope he is all you think him to be, but I do think you should
know all about his past.  You may suddenly find yourself in love with
him and then what?  You will soon be eighteen and a woman grown.  Old
enough to decide matters for yourself.  I hope you don't think me
meddling when I insist on your being familiar with his family history
before you let your self in for a life of repentence."
     "Oh, Uncle Will, please don't worry about me.  I'm not in the
least concerned about matrimony," she said emphatically.
     "You shouldn't ignore the attentions of our home town boys.  You
refused to go again with Jerry Williams who is from one of Peoria's
best families.  There are no skeletons hid in their closets," he boasted.
     "I agree with you, Uncle Will, there are none hid but plenty at
large.  I have my own reasons for not caring for his company."
     It was time to return now and as they neared the shop Parepa said,
"Please, don't think me ungratful, Uncle Will, but I believe I am a
better reader of human nature than you are.  Thanks just the same for
your kind interest in me."
     "That's all right, and don't forget that you promised me two years
ago that you would come to me if you were in distress for any reason."
     "I'll keep my promise, Uncle Will.  You can depend on that."
