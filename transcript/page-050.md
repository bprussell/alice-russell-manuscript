     "I haven't decided exactly but I am going to make a thorough
study of sociology first.  Will keep up piano and voice, too."
     "We'll all miss you when you leave," said Mr. Slay.

     "And you have made my time here pleasant by taking me under your
wing.  Isn't Parepa an interesting girl though.  So different from
any I ever met.  I like her immensely.  When I come back she will be
changed I suppose.  Maybe not one bit like the Parepa we know today."
"I don't believe she will ever change.  Only, of course, she will be
more matured  --  more sophisticated, but her pure loving heart will
never change," and Aunt Kate said it as if she were gazing into the
future.
     "You are the fellow that will be changed," accused Uncle George,
point a finger at Frances.  "The next five or six years of your life
will be years of continual changes.  I doubt if you will even think of
us after you are gone from her."
     "I give you both my promise - as long as I live I shall never
forget you and your hospitality.  Do you know, or can you believe,
that I was never in a real home before in my life?  I mean a home where
there was a husband and wife.  All my life I have lived in hotels or
on my private estates where everything is done so formally by servants
and caretakers.  My guardian is the only real "heart" friend I have.
He is a Prince of men.  No mother could have left her child in better
hands.  He is carrying out her dying requests to the letter; he lives
for me and me only.    It's a shame, you see his grandfather was my
grandmother's guardian, his father was my mother's guardian, and now he is
my guardian.  I love him more than life itself.  He and I are working
out a great plan by which we can be most helpful in the world." he
