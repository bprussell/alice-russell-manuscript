     Time went on - the crops were in and up.  Prospects were good up
to the middle of June.  Dry weather set in, and stayed in all summer.
Grains averaged about one half crop.  Prices remained low.  The "golden
harvest" seemed anything but.
     Allie's garden suffered many casualities through the summer that
would try the sturdiest heart.  In spite of this fact she managed by
the kindly thoughtfulness of dear friends who donated to her all
their surplus blooms,to satisfactorily fill all orders.
     The debts which they had hoped to pay with crop returns must re-⟦hyphen⟧
main unpaid another year.  Johnie and Allie sat on their back-porch this
beautiful fall evening trying to think out the best way to face the
matter,when all at once Allie jumped to her feet with an exclamation of
joy,and taking Johnie's head between her two hands she turned his face
toward the west.  "Look," she cried, "look at that beautiful sunset
after this gloomy, rainy day.  Isn't it wonderful?"
     Johnie pulled her down on his lap and kissing her said, in his
kindly way.  "Yes, wifie, it is beautiful.  Do you suppose our gloomy
rainy life will end in a sunset as glorious as that?"
