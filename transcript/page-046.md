clouds burst and a torrent ensued.
     The poor girl sobbed over little Eva and Uncle Tom  --  until
Uncle George kindly put his arm around her and drawing her head to his
shoulder tried to whisper comforting things inher ear.  He told her to
forget they were in the Opera House and just close her eyes and try to
sleep.  She had no luck, however, in taking his advice and would break
out spontaneously in a roar of sobs that attracted more attention than
the main actor on the stage.
     "Let's take the child home, it's getting on her nerves," whispered
Aunt Kate to Frances who immediately and willingly agreed.
     They made quite a commotion in leaving the box as Parepa's sobs
rose louder and louder and her efforts to control them seemed only to
aggravate.  She was so ashamed and depressed.  She had spoiled the
show for them all and for once, and only once, since she came to Peoria
had she wished she was home.
