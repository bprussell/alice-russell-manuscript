Submitted by:
Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
Mt. Hawley Road
Edelstein, Illinois

on the second floor⟦strikeover: letter overtyped in "floor"⟧ with ample closet space and lots of windows.  Of all
the inquiries Johnie and Allie liked the two young men from Boston, who

were employed by the government in the after war reconstruction work at
the Holt Manufacturing Company.  These young fellows had been staying
at one of the best hotels in the City and begged Allie to give them
breakfast, pack a lunch and serve them an evening dinner.  For this they
offerel what seemed to Johnie and Allie a huge sum.  Allie was afraid
to tackle it.  She thought her way of cooking was countrified and told
them so.

     They both decalred their willingness to risk it.  So it was settled,
Monday morning found them "moved in".  It was a lot of extra work for
Allie but she undertook it cheerfully.  The young men, Mr Rice and Mr.
Brown were more than pleased with the homey atmosphere of the place
and bragged continually on the fine meals served them.

     Johnie and Allie decided to make the thirty-five dollars per week
paid by them keep the table for the seven of them.  Allie run a bill
at the corner grocery store and paid every Saturday night.  Things were
at the high peek at that time and try as she would the thirty-five
dollars would not reach.  She always bought what she knew to be good
value and such nurishing foods as she wanted her family to enjoy.

     Butter was seventy-five cents a pound, sugar if you could get it
at all was "out of sight".  No good meat was less than fifty cents a
pound, milk⟦strikeover: "k" overtyped⟧ was sixteen cents a quart, flour was two dollars and fifteen
cents for a twenty-four pound sack, eggs, potatoes and fruits were
accordingly high.  It was not unsual to have to add an extra ten or
fifteen to the thirty-five.  Thus, costing an average of about seven and
one half dollars per person per week.

     They were all a healthy happy⟦strikeover: "h" overtyped⟧ family.  Month after month passed.
