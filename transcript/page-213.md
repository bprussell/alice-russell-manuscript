     She walked to another window and looked out.  Yes, she was alone,
there, by ⟦pencil-insert: the barn,⟧ stands her husband and his three fine sons which she had given
him,talking, planning and co-operating together.  She was alone.  They
are all men - they can't understand, and for a short moment tears sprang
to her eyes.  She quickly wiped them away.  My garden! my flowers! she
cried.  They are my lovely daughters!  They are my companions.  I'll
nurse them, I'll carress them, I'll raise them up into beautiful char-⟦hyphen⟧
acters, I'll talk to them, I'll play with them, I'll train them, I'll
teach them right from wrong.  Oh, we will be the best companions!  They
shall be as good adn grand as my wonderful sons.  I shall be satisfied.
I shall give to my world two rare gifts, homest uprgith men, beautiful,
understanding flowers.  My gifts - to my God.  And she "Thanked God For
a Garden!"
