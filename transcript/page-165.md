                              CHAPTER I

     Parepa went back to the farm; the cows, the hills, the dity ⟦pencil: dirty⟧ dishes,
and the big swing.  Back to the home where dwelt the loving mother and
father who did not "shatter her to bits" and "remould" her.  Back to
the real brothers and sisters whose blood she knew was pure.
     Isabel, the eldest child of the Wesley family was teaching a country
school.  Lavena, the next oldest sister was her father's and mother's
'right hand man' taking the responsibility of the younger children onto
her own shoulders so completely that they felt her 'orders were orders'
and stepped to her music or wished they had.  She was a -born mamager
and managed the whole family.  The boys were regular farmers, working
early and late day after day.
     Little Iona the baby of the family was now twelve years old and
to her "Allie's" heart went out at dish-washing and milking time.
"Allie" was none other than Alice Parepa-Roza.  "The "Allie" was the
nickname for Alice, given her at home by her parents when she was
still in "tothood".  The only time the full name of Alice Parepa-Roza
was used at home was when it was necessary to make it forceful - as it
was in the first lines written in this story.  The family in general
as well as the home neighborhood knew her as Allie, the "different"
Wesley girl.  The neighborhood hadn't missed her much, these four years -
"she was rather odd you know" - and so her advent into the commu ity
again was unheralded and unsung.  She crept into the scheme of things
just as she would have chosen to do had she her choice.
