                              CHAPTER XVIII

     At promptly five-thirty Frances pulled up in front of the Slay
home with a beautifully matched span of coal black beauties.  Prepa
was ready and Aunt Kate stood on the porch and waved a long good-bye as
they cantered down the shady street.
     "We'll have two hours before dusk," said Frances.  "Which way shall
we go?"
     "Oh, let's ride all through Glen Oak Park.  I never get tired of
that place.  It's heaven to me and it's shady there on the drives," she
replied.
     "Fine!  I like it too," and he pulled his horse closer to hers.
     "There isn't so much riding in this country as in England," he said.
     "No, it isn't a national sport," she said.  "Tell me about England
and what you do there.  Are we so very different?"
     "There's only one thing that's really so <u>very</u> different as I've
found, and that's - well, that's you," he hesitated.
     "Me!  Am I really a novelty then?  May be I should have gone on the
stage after all.  Be in a cage, you know, and charge so much to see me,"
she teased.
     "No, you shouldn't let any one see you but me.  I was so jealous
the other night when everyone went crazy about you, I hardly knew how to
control myself.  Couldn't bear the thoughts of your making a show for
the amusement of hundreds who didn't know what a sweet thing you were.
You'll never do that again, will you?" he begged.
     "Did I really do so wrong?  Did I cheapen myself and disgrace my
friends?  Really did I?  I only wanted to surprize you all, but not one
of my dearest friends have offered congratulations."
