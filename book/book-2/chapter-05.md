# Chapter V

Parepa could hear a low argument going on outside but could not see the parties taking part in it. However, she was soon to be introduced to the main actor.

Harold broke past the boy who was trying to persuade him to go to his room and sober up before letting Parepa see him. "She's my girl and I'll see her if I want to," he stammered in a voice not at all familiar to Parepa.

Before she could turn to see him he fell over her chair with arms around her and, kissing her cheek, he said, "Dish is my sweetheart and nobody can stop me from seeing her. Yer glad I come ain't you, honey? Ain't you now?" and he gave an ugly lunge over her shoulder again, "Give me a sweet kish, honey."

Parepa beat him off and all the boys at the table rose to her defense and drug him from the house and down the street. He was angry drunk. The first person Parepa had ever seen in that condition.

"What is the matter with him, is he sick?" she asked in blank surprise.

"Yes, drunk sick," said the old maid teacher who sat at the head of the table. "We all wanted to tell you but none of us had the nerve. It isn't his first offense by any means."

"You mean—you mean he gets drunk?" and she fell forward. She had fainted.

The girls all rushed to help her. They laid her on Mrs. Allen's bed and called a doctor. "It was a terrible shock to the child," Mrs. Allen told him. "Harold has told the boys they are to be married at the end of the term."

"He is such a fine smart fellow when he is sober, but a regular demon when he is drunk."

Parepa "came to" enough to hear this much of the conversation then lapsed into unconsciousness again. The doctor gave them strict orders not to let her meet the young man again for several days. It was an hour before Parepa was able to be moved to her own room across the street. Loving hands did everything that could be done for her physical comfort and kind words breathed tenderly into her ears the heartfelt messages of true girl friends. They all loved her. She was so good.

She remained in her room the next day. Harold was back to his old self again and missed his sweetheart. Some of the "spunky" girls told him what he had done and how he had broken Parepa's heart. They were merciless in their condemnation. He flinched under the lash of their words.

"Believe me girls I would rather have died than to have had that thing happen. It's the first time since I've known her and it will be the last time in my life. I give you my word it's the last time."

They were touched by his emotional statement. They all liked him so much when he was himself. "It's too bad," was all they could say. He wanted the girls to take messages to Parepa but they refused. He called at her rooming house but the landlady would not admit him, saying it was Miss Wesley's request.

He wrote her a long penitent letter begging her forgiveness, promising never again to drink and telling her over and over again of his undying love for her. He begged her to see him just once again.

She crushed the letter in her hand and tossed it into the wastebasket. "I'll never speak to him again. Never."
