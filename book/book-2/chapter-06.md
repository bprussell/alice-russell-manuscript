# Chapter VI

In the evening mail came a letter from Johnie. Parepa pressed it to her lips. It was a plainly written letter. No extra flourishes, no learned language, no specimen penmanship, yet every word rang true. Parepa appreciated more than ever this friendship. "He has something no other of my friends have. Something I cannot explain. Something I want. Something I am going to have. I love him! Oh, God in heaven, I know I love him. It is he I have been feeling for—now I have found him! Oh, Johnie, why did I let you go home to keep on hoping—instead of giving myself to you. I am yours now—all yours. It is your blood that shall flow in my children's veins."

The first of September, 1904, found Allie unlocking the door of the old Kellogg schoolhouse again. She was a happy, contented girl now. Her whole ambition was in her work. She was bound to do her best this last year of teaching. They would be married next June after school had closed.

It was a most successful year in every way. Johnie and Allie were two happy lovers. Their hours together were spent for the most part planning their home. Johnie's father, having been an excellent carpenter in his working days, set about to rebuild the old house which stood on a slight slope of a hill on one of the Bradford farms.

It was completed now and ready for the final decorative touch of the painter and paperhanger. Such a dream of a cottage, and such fun to plan every detail of arrangement of furniture, garden, orchard, and lawn.
