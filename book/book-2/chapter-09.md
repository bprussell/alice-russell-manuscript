# Chapter IX

The time came when she was unable to help with the store duties. Her sister Iona came to help them out and was a great comfort to them both.

Allie found time now to put a few dainty touches on the baby's clothes and basket. She found it hard to wait for its arrival. They were both so happy. Finally the warning was sounded and the nurse, Doctor and a neighbor lady summoned.

For hours upon hours the young mother suffered. No less a sufferer was her husband. At eleven-thirty a.m. on June the fourteenth, 1910, there burst on the ether ladened air the cry of a new born babe. "It's a boy John and a big fellow," the old Doctor announced as he handed him over to the nurse.

It was some time before Allie realized what had happened. When she came to, a husky little red headed baby was laying by her side.

"Guess what he is, a girl or a boy?" said the happy father.

"He's a girl, of course," she answered weakly smiling up at her husband.

"You are wrong, he's our boy," and he stooped to kiss his wife, the wonderful mother of his wonderful son. When she grew stronger a name was chosen—Robert Frances.

Allie didn't know why the Frances came so naturally to her mind. She had always liked that name she told herself and that must be the reason.

No baby ever entered this world more welcome than did this red-faced squirming little body. From the first peek at him his father's heart was won.

"Isn't he a cute little bugger?" he'd say as he watched him sleeping in his basket. "How did we ever get along those five years without him, anyway?"

Allie loved to care for him. He was a good baby. They had lots to be thankful for. When he was three months old Allie would take him to the store where she would "help out" awhile each day. As he grew older Johnie made a pen to keep him in at the store so he wouldn't get into mischief.

He grew so chubby and rosy. Always happy and smiling. He became a great favorite with old and young alike. When he was three years old a baby brother came to claim the same father and mother.

Richard LaVerne was the name he answered to. Allie didn't know why they named him LaVerne unless it was because she had always liked it.

He was a fine child, dark hair like his father's with a touch of hazel in his eyes. He was gladly welcomed into the family circle. He soon identified himself by his strong aversion to nature's food.

Nothing seemed to agree with him. For one whole year he gave his parents a great deal of worry. They had traded the store for a stock and fruit farm now and were trying to make the interest on the mortgage by raising chickens, milking cows and selling fruit. Richard flourished on the milk from the herd and grew into a chubby laughing two year old.

In April, 1915 another baby boy put in his appearance bidding for a share of the family's love and attention. Arthur Emmerson—Allie didn't know just why the Emmerson unless it was because she liked that name.

He had a head of thick red hair just as Robert came into the world with. In fact he was just like Robert in every way.

Johnie was so disappointed in his not being the girl he had so much wanted. He sniffled considerable before he could overcome his disappointment enough to tell Allie it was another boy.

"I don't care just so he's all right," she said and thanked God she had no reason to want to change his sex.

Things had drifted into a regular routine at the Bradford home. It was always a rush and a hurry from one duty to another both for the husband with the farm responsibilities and for the wife with children and household to look after.

These parents loved their little boys and carefully raised them as they should be raised. Nothing concerning their welfare was neglected as far as they were financially and physically able to supply it. They were not over indulgent parents. There must be a good reason for every purchase. Their food, clothing and even their play things were discussed and their practical value to the child in question made certain before money was spent for it.

These were strenuous days. John was doing his best to fight off a return of his old trouble. Finally his Doctor told him he must make a change. As long as he was on the farm he would work too hard and not rest as he should. Regular hours was what he needed.
