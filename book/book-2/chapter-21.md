# Chapter XXI

It had all been so exciting from the beginning that Allie had not had time to consider the seriousness of her mission. She bought her ticket to Chicago. From there she would take a through Pullman to Tucson on the Rock Island. After goodbyes were said, and she was seated on the train she pinched herself to see if she were dreaming. Things she had forgotten to tell the home folks to do ran through her brain. She pulled a tablet and pencil from her bag and started a letter to them. Finally she chucked ten pages of letter into a shabby envelope and gave it to the porter to mail for her. She addressed it to Robert and disguised her handwriting. In that letter she told them that hereafter she would address their letters to Mr. J.S. Bradford, General Delivery, Peoria, Illinois, and that they were to go to the Peoria Post Office and call for them. She explained that she thought best not to send letters to Edelstein for a while.

She then worded a telegram to Daddy Wheeler. Allie laughed to herself as she took stock of the contents of her traveling bag. There were only two dresses, three pair of silk stockings which were Christmas presents, three suits of underwear, a nightgown, an old silk kimona, two under slips, a dozen handkerchiefs, a new pair of shoes which she had never worn, stamps, tablet, envelopes, comb, powder, and a copy of the latest magazine, which Johnie had thoughtfully stuck in at the last minute.

She carried with her a large pillow and a pretty dark blue plaid blanket—another Christmas present. After changing cars in Chicago she was so sleepy and tired that she decided to curl up on her berth and go to sleep. This she did. Without any difficulty whatever she was soon asleep. Such is the reward of a clear conscience. When she awoke it was to hear the call for dinner. She had missed her breakfast but felt no worse for it.

She went to the dressing room and cleaned herself up. She really looked refreshed and attractive as she sat at the table gazing through the window deep in study. She had a knack of making her clothes look as if made by the most popular designer. There was always an original touch that gave them that out-of-the-ordinary look. That she was an intelligent woman was plainly evident by the alert and interested expression that shone from her eyes and refined features of her face.

Unknowingly she was being studied by the other occupants of the dining car who mentally attributed her light luncheon to a desire to reduce. It never occurred to her admirers that the desire to reduce was limited to the reduction of cost, not foods. It seemed to Allie she had never eaten such good food. She left the table only half satisfied. She was still hungry. No one knew it though, and she would try to forget it by writing a letter home.

After paying for her ticket she had exactly twenty-one dollars and fourteen cents left. Not much to go on, that was sure, but she would make it do. She wrote long and interestingly to her husband and children.

She read over and over the pages of her magazine more to have her mind employed than because it was interesting. She did not care to become communicative with those around her though they tried every way within propriety to get better acquainted with her.

The second day she found herself being really annoyed by a portly, red faced gentleman who insisted upon waiting on her in every way possible. He followed closely by her side to the dining car and showed her a seat taking the one across from her. Their conversation was not interesting to Allie. When the waiter came to their table the stranger said, "I'm giving this order for two," and smiling at Allie added, "Have you a choice?"

"None at all, thank you," she answered not caring to argue with him.

He gave a most elaborate order. After the waiter left he addressed his companion thusly. "Now, I think we should introduce ourselves, say you?"

"I beg your pardon but I am making no new acquaintances on this trip, and boasted on the start that no one should break my resolution. I hope you don't think me rude," she said decidedly.

"Well, maybe not rude, but rather peculiar. I am disappointed. I had looked forward to a great deal of friendly companionship together. As for me I have no cause to hide my identity," he added a bit sarcastically.

"Well, I don't mind telling you that I have. I'll appreciate your not urging me to disclose it. Let's talk about the weather or something."

"All right," he agreed, "what kind of weather do you have at home?" he asked teasingly.

"Oh, changeable," she laughed. "And sometimes *very* unusual."

"Must be you are from Florida or California," he said. "Which?"

"Ask me another, as my boys would say," she answered glad to let him know she was a mother of boys.

"Your boys?" he said as if he had caught her in a bad break.

"Surely, three of them. All as big as their mother and the finest in the world. Their Daddy and I are so proud of them. How many have you got?" she quizzed.

"I'm afraid I can't tell as big a one as you have. I haven't any. Never did have." Allie thought his face saddened. She would change the subject.

"Isn't this scenery beautiful, this time of the year?" she asked. "I love the Springtime."

"I love it too, it's my favorite season. So filled with hope and promise," he said.

"Yes, but I have found that Fall don't always fulfill Spring's promise," she replied. "So I figure we should enjoy Spring while we have it."

"That's just what I set out to do on this trip," he said. "No matter what Fall might bring I'm going to enjoy the Spring." They chatted idly through the meal. When she thanked him for it he said, "The thanks should go to you. The pleasure has been mine. I'll leave you now so you can write to those three wonderful boys of yours," he chided.

"And to the best husband in the world," she added. "Goodbye."

After she was seated she thought over the doings of her last hour. It had been unavoidable without making a scene. She hoped he would not repeat it. She took her writing material from her bag and wrote a long letter home. She felt a bit lonely but was too brave to acknowledge it.

She curled up on the seat and covering herself with her blanket went to sleep. She did not care for the evening meal, so remained in her seat. Another day was past and she welcomed the night. She wished her trip was over.

For the first time since she started on her journey had she considered the meeting with Frances and Daddy Wheeler. Would they be changed? Would they be disappointed in her? What would she do, how would she act? It wasn't going to be easy. She wished Johnie was with her. Why was a good person like Frances taken out of the world in his prime? Joseph Donald had been called away, too. Never once did her mind wonder or speculate on the financial possibilities. After all, why had she come? What impulse urged her to make this trip? Would she admit it? Yes, it was the desire to see once again her old sweetheart. Her first love of maidenhood days. Her heart beat rapidly as she remembered their innocent love. She basked in the memory of it all. The first time in her married life she allowed herself to indulge to the utmost in reminiscence of her first love. Now, Johnie knew the secret that had been hidden and guarded so safely for years in her heart. He had never dreamed of there being such a thing. Would there be an unfavorable reaction? Would he mistrust her when the excitement of present events is over? Would she lose any of her children's respect when they knew?

As the train neared the end of the journey Allie was breathless with the charm of the beautiful scenery. The ride through this Spanish Western country seemed to take her into another world. The desert cactus, the irrigated orange groves, the Indian villages, the unusual enchanting picturesque scenery and great open spaces all held a gripping western appeal. The rugged, jagged mountain peaks especially interested her. It seemed she was entering a land of Romance—passing from an old world into a new.

Now there was deep chasms and canyons—now sunny gardens of green and gold—silver gray desert stretches—towering forms of cactus giants—and *color*! Oh the beautiful blendings of color! Could the Temple of Solomon have been grander? Nowhere had she found nature's colors so striking. Our old Parepa-Roza had come back! Here, in the same seat and even in the same body in which Allie had entered the train, was this beauty worshiper of other days. Not one aspect of enchanting charm escaped the eyes of this woman. They were saturated with its loveliness.

It was wonderful. Her thoughts traveled homeward to her husband. How he would have enjoyed this trip. How he loved the Colorado Mountains. "Well, someday," she said to herself, "we will take this trip together." She took her tablet from her bag, wrote her last long letter en route to her home folks.

It was three-thirty o'clock in the afternoon when she left the train. She was just entering the depot when a young man dressed in livery stepped to her side and touching her on the arm asked, "Are you Mrs Bradford from Illinois?"

"I am," she answered.

"Well, Mr. Wheeler is waiting for you in the car," he said, and taking her shabby looking bag from her hand added, "Come with me."

She was glad she had taken a good deal of time with her toilet on the train. She really looked quite presentable. Her cheeks wore the natural flush which gave a sparkle to her eyes. Her dimples were the same as when a child. She was excited and animated adding to her attractiveness.
