# Chapter II

Allie was surprised that she should stand any show at all. She had received no encouragement from any of the home folks in this "crazy venture" of hers. They couldn't see how she could expect to teach school after being out of school so long.

Nevertheless, the first Monday in September found her unlocking the door of the old Kellogg schoolhouse, where her father had attended school when a youngster and where her sister Isabel had "served time" before her.

It was a small school of only eight pupils, but it kept the teacher busy just the same. She realized more and more how little she knew. She boarded in a home that joined the school ground. Worked for part of her board bill. Her wages were thirty five dollars a month. Her schoolroom soon took on a "pretty" look. The superintendent commented favorably on it, saying he wished all his teachers were as artistic.

She was determined to save every cent she could, that she might attend normal school that summer, one way or another.

Most of the young swains had overstepped their bounds and had been more or less honorably discharged. The only one left that Allie really cared for was the son of a Scotch family living just south of the schoolhouse. Allie never could figure out why it should have been he who was to fall prey to her "Endearing Young Charms."

He was not one bit like the rest of them. He wasn't so "up and comin'." Was more modest in his demonstrations of affection, and at times seemed almost impossible when his backwardness, yes, and awkwardness, embarrassed her almost past redemption. He had several finer qualities to recommend him, though, and Allie was not slow to appreciate them. His genuine kindliness, his unpolished, truthful standards, his dogged perseverance in anything he undertook, and his unlimited powers of endurance. He was staunch and true always. So it was that on the evening before Allie left for the State Normal University at Bloomington, Illinois, Johnie got up his courage to propose to her.

His love making had nothing about it that reminded her of her first love. His was a love as natural and simple as the call of a bird to its mate. There were no false notions in this young man's head. He felt the urge of real love prompting him to reveal it to his sweetheart, which he did, simply and honestly.

While she had not admitted it even to herself, she had gathered great respect and appreciation for his unwavering steadfastness. She hardly believed herself in love with this unassuming young country boy. That was hardly what she'd call it. She didn't know just how to describe it even to herself. Yet there was something—what was it—anyway?

He was so very uneducated, not especially attractive, and not as well groomed as most of the young fellows in their crowd. Allie was not sure that it wasn't all these things she admired most in him. He did nothing to mislead her, yet she felt he understood her better than anyone else.

After talking it over they decided to let the matter rest until she returned from school. Perhaps she would know her mind better by that time. They'd just keep on being good friends for a while.
