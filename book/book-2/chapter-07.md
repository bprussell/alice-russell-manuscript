# Chapter VII

June 21st, 1905, was a day long looked forward to and its setting sun witnessed the uniting of two hearts and lives—for better or for worse—for richer or for poorer—in sickness and in health until death do them part.

This happy couple met and overcame the many unexpected matrimonial intricacies which are bound to arise in all well regulated families. By the end of the third year they were nearly out of debt and owned a fair equipment both of household furnishings and farm machinery. Allie was fortunate in winning a piano in a contest which she made good use of. However, she never sang. They had accumulated considerable livestock of which they were justly proud. Each was interested in everything that concerned the other. Both were working hard and enjoying every bit of it.

Fall came—the ripened corn hung from its husks on the tall stalks in the fields as though beckoning the harvester to gather them in. Allie loved to help her swarthy companion pile high in the wagon the golden ears. They visited and planned as they worked. One day Allie was sure there was something worrying Johnie. He wasn't himself. What could be on his mind? She wouldn't ask him. He would tell her soon.

Each day as they worked side by side she noticed a decided decrease in pep and vim, both in his actions and conversation. There was an unmistakable "slowing up" of everything. Something terrible is on his mind, thought Allie as she glanced sideways at him slowly pulling the husks from the corn.

"Johnie!" she cried as she realized the truth. "Johnie! What do you mean by keeping this from me? You are ill, so ill you can hardly stand up." As she said this he staggered toward the wagon and leaning against it said, "I was anxious to get through first," then he dropped in a heap on the ground.

"Oh, Allie! I am so sick, dearie, so sick." After he rested awhile she helped him into the wagon and taking the lines in her hands drove the team to the barn. She helped him to bed and called the doctor. Before he arrived Johnie told her how he had known for days that something was holding him back. Shortness of breath, dizziness, etc. At first he had hoped to wear it off. He did so want to finish the corn picking. Then he could rest all winter.

Allie scolded him for not telling her and scolded herself for not suspecting.

"I thought you were worrying about something. I couldn't imagine what. It never entered my head until this morning that you were sick," she said patting him on the cheek.

"I was worrying about you darling. I didn't want to give up."
