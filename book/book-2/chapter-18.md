# Chapter XVIII

This was the time that was usually most welcomed by Johnie and Allie. This year was different. They knew not which way to turn. The sheriff had made his second call and his mission meant but one thing to these honest folk. They must give up.

"But what will we do?" asked Robert. "The factories are laying off men every day and there is no opening for any untrained men now." Richard was quiet but thinking. Arthur would have to come home from high school. They all realized it would be impossible to pay his board any longer.

Johnie and Allie lay awake long nights talking together and trying to come to some conclusion as to the future residence of their family. It was January, 1932, now. In two months they must be off their farm which they all loved so well. It would be rented to some other farmer. Johnie had told the owner he was unable to hold out any longer. Allie hoped they would take good care of her garden. Day after day they made trips to Peoria hoping to find a place in which they could afford to live. Robert watched the "help wanted" ads and applied for work only to find each time that a dozen or more were ahead of him.

One evening they sat at the supper table a silent downhearted family each wrapped in their own gloomy thoughts. Even Allie with her optimistic nature had no solution to the problem over which each was pondering. Bankruptcy was not only staring them in the face but was actually hissing at them. Again the familiar lines ran through her mind:

> "Ah, love could you and I with fate conspire,  
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To grasp this sorry scheme of things entire;  
> Would we not shatter it to bits and then,  
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Remould it nearer to the heart's desire?"

She sat thoughtlessly drawing a picture on the white tablecloth. A huge locket! She recognized it at once, What made her do it! Why should this thing come to haunt her at this unwelcome time?
