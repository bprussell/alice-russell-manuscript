# Chapter XXVII

That night Parepa slept like a child. At nine o'clock the next morning a tap on the door and her maid announced Daddy Wheeler. He looked composed and well groomed. Parepa thought perhaps he was going to suggest a shopping trip to the City this beautiful morning. He greeted her with a smile and a kiss.

"Are you as fine as the day my dear?" he asked drawing her close in his arms.

"Yes, Daddy, I wouldn't know how to act if I felt any better," she laughed.

"That's good," and helping her to a chair he drew another closer. Reaching for her hand he said, "Frances left us this morning, Parepa. She has gone to her God. All is well. All is well."

Parepa could only stare at this wonderfully poised old man. This man who was resignation personified. Could she control herself as this fine example before her? Yes, she would. Frances would want her to. It was her way. She must live up to the standard set by this bereft old gentleman. Strangely enough Parepa had no desire to weep again.

The plans were made and every wish was carried out.

As Parepa-Roza took her place beside Daddy Wheeler, and the little procession moved slowly from the sobered "Kashema," the setting sun tinted the skies with a thousand hues of red, gold, and purple. Then the night—the desert moon and stars swing like hanging lamps—so near to the earth that they seemed almost to touch the thorny fingers of the stately old mountains that stood as watchmen of the dunes.

The beauty and sublimity of the soft velvet night saturated with the perfume ladened air of the desert beneath the mysterious expanse of its star studded sky seemed to soften the sorrow and loneliness in the hearts and minds of the earthly companions of the departed one as the funeral cortege passed into the little grave yard.

Reverently as though a throng of mourners were weeping beside the bier, the body of one whose life had blessed the earth, was laid to rest.

The last link in the Emmerson chain was broken. They had taken their long ride.
