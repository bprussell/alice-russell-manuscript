# Chapter XXII

As they neared the limousine she recognized the calm face of Daddy Wheeler. He was standing straight and tall beside the car looking toward them. He removed his hat and waved at her. When she came nearer he met her with outstretched arms. He kissed her cheek and greeted her with warm words of welcome. There was nothing offensive about this grand old man in his eighties.

He was the picture of good health and right living, well preserved in every way. His handclasp was strong and his cheery words, "Welcome my daughter," would have been a shock to Parepa had they come from anyone else. After the driver assisted them to their seat in the car, Mr. Wheeler said, "You better give him your baggage checks and he will get it for you."

Parepa blushed as she told him she had none. "This is all I brought. I can only stay a few days you know." The subject was dropped but she thought Daddy looked amused. As they rode through the city of Tucson Allie thought she never saw so much beauty. Daddy Wheeler keenly noted her surprise and delight as he pointed out to her many interesting places. Old missions, prehistoric ruins, the great Pioneer Hotel, the Pepper Tree Inn, the eucalyptus trees, the towering peaks of the Santa Ritas and Mesa Verde Range. And the sunshine! Was she to become a sun worshiper too? They rode for several miles in the country, then all at once on a desert knoll there appeared an impressive structure of Spanish architecture, conforming to no very definite school of design.

She knew without being told that this was Frances's home.

"There it is," she said. "I know it is."

"You are right, but how did you know?" he asked pleased.

"It is just what I expected to find," she answered. "It is beautiful and liveable looking yet not in the least pretentious. That is just like Frances would want it."

"Then you haven't forgotten?" her companion asked, a pleased expression crossing his face.

"Not one thing, Daddy Wheeler, not one minute," she answered.

When they entered the long drive she noticed that all the shrubs except those in the little gem of a patio were native to the desert and the color of the home itself blended harmoniously into the sun-baked desert tan. "Oh, it is beautiful!" she exclaimed. "I can see Frances's nature cropping out all over. I hope he is able to enjoy it for many years to come."

"She is not apt to my dear unless seeing you works a miracle. She has rallied considerable since we got your telegram. I am glad you lost no time in coming," he said and Parepa noticed his face cloud as he added, "Her one hope seemed to be that she find you before it was too late."

"She?" questioned Parepa and the old man knew what she meant.

"Yes," he said, "when this disease fastened itself upon her five years ago we transferred all our business interests to the United States and when we arrived here she changed her sex. We immediately set about to change all financial interests into cash, sometimes at a great loss, and to place funds on deposit in the banks of the largest cities in the United States. This was done to simplify matters in transferring it to you. Frances has planned everything. It will be an easy thing to sign it over."

"Please don't say any more about that now. I came because I wanted to see him again," she pleaded.

The car stopped beside the step and they were let into the house by a porter. A kindly old lady came forward and was introduced to Parepa. She was the housekeeper. "I'll show you to your suite," she said and Parepa followed her. When she reached the top of the stair she was met by a French girl who was to be Parepa's maid.

"I'll draw your bath and send your clothes down to be pressed," she said sweetly as Parepa passed through her most embarrassing moment.

"I'm afraid I haven't but two dresses to be pressed," she confessed. "You see I came on a moment's notice and threw what few things I could grab into my bag in a hurry."

"Oh," she said and taking the travel soiled dress which Parepa drew from the bag left the room. She soon returned and drawing aside the curtains that separate the bath from her dressing room motioned Parepa to the tub and gave her the most enjoyable bath she ever had. It was scientifically done. After that was over she massaged her thoroughly from her feet to her very scalp. Her hands were manicured, her hair beautifully dressed, her body perfumed and powdered, and after an hour and a half of renovating she was ready to be presented to her first love.

Daddy Wheeler waited at the foot of the stairs for her. He took her by the arm and led her into a large living room at the far end of which reclining on a luxurious lounge lay Frances.
