# Reading-layer edits log

Log of corrections and editorial decisions made between the archival layer
(`transcript/`) and the reading layer (`book/`). Per-page corrections get
logged here as Phase 3 proceeds; global decisions are recorded up front.

## Global decisions

- **"Johnie" vs "Johnnie" (decided 2026-07-08, with the author's
  great-grandson):** the reading text normalizes the name to a single
  spelling, preferring the manuscript's most common form — **"Johnie"**
  (single n). Tally across all final post-correction readings: "Johnie"
  ≈ 90 occurrences over ~45 pages (173–276); "Johnnie" 9 occurrences,
  confined to pages 176–180 (one clean-typed plus eight pencil
  corrections adding the second "n"). The pencil "Johnnie" campaign was
  never extended beyond that five-page stretch, and on page 181 she
  struck typed "Johnnie" back to "Johnie" twice (once in blue pencil),
  so "Johnie" reads as her settled choice.

- **Book titles (decided 2026-07-08/09, with the author's great-grandson):**
  the manuscript's original titles were erased by the author. Both title
  blocks share a three-line centered "X / OF / Y" shape.
  - **Book I (page 1): "The Locket of Family Secrets" — composed.** The
    erased block survives only as "_____ / OF / _____ SECRETS" (the "OF"
    and "SECRETS" recovered by contrast enhancement; ghost-line width
    shows a ~5-7 letter word preceded "SECRETS"; the first line is fully
    lost). "The Locket" and "Family" are editorial choices fitted around
    the recovered fragments — the locket being the story's literal vessel
    of the Emmerson family's secrets. This is the edition's one truly
    invented title element, to be disclosed in the foreword.
  - **Book II (page 165): "The Heir of the Broken Chain" — reconstructed.**
    During the physical-page review the family recovered partial letters
    from the erased block: "____IR / __ / TH_ BR____ C__IN". The last
    line reads "THE BROKEN CHAIN" letter-for-letter — matching the
    manuscript's closing line ("The last link in the Emmerson chain was
    broken.") and page 162's vow that the child "shall break this line of
    weakness and shame." The first line's "...IR" ending fits "THE HEIR"
    (and Allie is Emmerson's heir in the plot). "THE BROKEN CHAIN" is
    treated as solidly recovered; "THE HEIR" as probable reconstruction.
  - The **foreword will explain** both cases: the originals were erased,
    Book II's title was substantially recovered from ghost impressions,
    and Book I's was composed around its two recovered words — the
    edition's one truly editorial invention.

- **Surname "Slay" normalized:** the one "Mr. Saly" (p26) is corrected to
  "Mr. Slay", the spelling used everywhere else in the manuscript.
- **"convenience/convenienced" for "convince(d)" is KEPT as voice:** it
  occurs three times across the manuscript (pp24, 26, 79), consistently —
  the author's settled word, not a one-off slip.
- **"Nanettezen" normalized (2026-07-20, dominant-form rule):** the
  maid's name is typed four ways (Nanettezen 2x, Nanettzen/Nanettezon/
  Nanettezan 1x each); the reading text uses "Nanettezen". Needs a match
  in Chapter XXIV (p264).
- **"Anna Bell" normalized (2026-07-20, dominant-form rule):** the
  child's mother's name is typed variously "Anna Bell"/"Annabell"/
  "Annabel"; the reading text uses "Anna Bell", the dominant form
  (including her signature "Anna Bell Emmerson" on p124), following the
  same dominant-form normalization already applied to Johnie/Slay.
- **"surry/surries" → "surrey/surreys" (decided 2026-07-09, reviewer):**
  initially kept as an era variant, but "surrey" is the correct spelling
  of the carriage; corrected wherever it appears (pp37-area of ch. I,
  p18 twice).

- **Spelling of compounds modernized (decided 2026-10-11, with the
  author's great-grandson):** the reading text uses modern American
  spelling for words she typed split or hyphenated — "never-the-less" →
  "nevertheless", "bath room" → "bathroom", "street car" → "streetcar",
  "spell bound" → "spellbound", "good bye"/"good-bye" → "goodbye", "some
  one" → "someone", "some day" → "someday", "down town" → "downtown",
  "for awhile" → "for a while", etc. (about 230 changes across all 57
  chapters; not logged per instance). The aim is to remove distraction
  for today's reader; her words, grammar, and phrasing are untouched,
  and the verbatim transcripts preserve every original form. This
  supersedes the "era two-word forms" kept-as-voice notes in the
  per-chapter sections below. Applied case by case, not blindly — kept
  as two words where that is still correct or where joining would
  change the meaning:
  - grammatically separate uses: "any one bank", "every one of them",
    "in any way", "for some time", "some time ago", "any more calls",
    "rest awhile", "may be" (verb), "in to"/"on to", "every day"
    (adverb), "her heart beat rapidly" (verb), "watch the water fall",
    "until after noon", "a back seat", "all grown up", "some way";
  - "Monroe Street car tracks", "high school work", "Sunday School
    work/room", "Sunday school mate";
  - **"girl friend(s)"** (her female friends — "girlfriend" would imply
    romance), **"boy friend"** (Ch IV, p26, Frances on the day they
    meet; first joined in this pass, restored 2026-10-11 by the same
    reasoning) and **"love making"** (courtship — the modern closed form
    means something else), plus "master mind" and "life boat" (her
    figurative senses);
  - open compounds that are still standard today ("living room", "ice
    cream", "post office", "high school", "no one", "under way").
  Hyphenation of compound adjectives ("old fashioned", "good looking")
  was not part of this pass.

  with the author's great-grandson):** agreement slips in the narration

    Ch XXV typo "this world's good" → "goods" (quoted correctly a line
    earlier).
  - Book II: Ch I "There were the usual ice cream 'sociables'" and
    "every member of the family was seated"; Ch III "Her heart and mind
    were too full"; Ch IX "Regular hours were what he needed"; Ch XIII
    "there, by the barn, stand her husband and his three fine sons"; Ch
    XIV "sticktuitiveness and refusal... were most astonishing" and "The

    far as the return from the garden was concerned"; Ch XX "After the

  Reviewed and **kept**: Book I Ch II "milking the five cows which was
  her usual allotment" (the milking was her allotment); Ch IV '"He must
  be in by ten o'clock" was his guardian's strict orders'; Ch VII "the

- **Capitalization modernized (decided 2026-10-11, with the author's
  great-grandson):** she capitalized ordinary nouns she thought
  important ("went to Church", "the City", "the Doctor") — common in
  personal writing of her day, though publishers already lowercased
  them. The reading text now follows modern down style, in narration
  and dialogue alike (about 255 changes; found by a full read of every
  chapter, each case reviewed). **Lowercased:** church, city, hotel,
  opera/opera house, club, university, normal school, bank, post office,
  charities, the seasons, "Sunday school", job titles used as
  descriptions (doctor — 70 times — superintendent, principal, pastor,
  secretary, treasurer, manager), family words after a possessive or
  article ("her uncle and aunt", "their mother", "a spectacular daddy"),
  short forms of named places ("the club" for the Allen Club, "the
  hotel", "the opera house"), and stray emphatic capitals ("the Fire",
  "Peace and Quiet and Growth", "a Prince of men", "Oh" mid-sentence →
  "oh"). **Capitalized:** proper names she typed lowercase (Bethany
  Baptist Church, Bradley College, Glen Oak Park, Grand Opera House,
  Peoria Methodist Hospital, Kellogg School), "Dutch", "Fourth of
  July", "the West" as a region, and "Mother"/"Mamma" used as a name in
  direct address. **Kept:** proper names; titles before a name; family
  words used as names ("Uncle and Auntie found...", "Daddy" for Mr.
  Wheeler, "Mother felt she should go"); "Doctor" used as a name ("I'll
  do my best, Doctor", the nurse's "maybe Doctor will let you"); God,
  Lord, Heaven and pronouns for God exactly as she wrote them;
  "Realtor" (a trademark); the verbatim gift card ("To My Dear Little
  Girl. A Rose for every year...") and the children's rhyme ("Work while
  you work, Play while you play...") as quoted. Hyphenated in passing:
  "morocco-bound" (twice). Three emphatic capitals became italics, the
  modern way to carry the same weight: Ch XXI "to Someone; love
  personified" → "to *someone*, love personified"; Ch XXX "Darling, I'm
  a Girl." → "Darling, I'm a *girl*."; and plain lowercase for Ch XXX
  "—My Dreams are Over." → "—my dreams are over."

- **"Mrs" and "Mr" take a period (decided 2026-11-10, with the author's
  great-grandson):** she typed "Mrs" without a period 73 times and with
  one 15 times, and "Mr" almost always with one; the reading text uses
  American "Mrs." and "Mr." throughout (75 periods added). Extends the
  earlier "Mr Rice" → "Mr. Rice" ruling (Book II Ch X).
