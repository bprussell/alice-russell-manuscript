# Chapter XI

The fall and winter passed quietly at the Slay home. Spring came and a trip home for two weeks was the only variation in Parepa's routine. She enjoyed her visit among home folks immensely, went for the cows, helped milk them, sang to them the same as she use to, visited her old schoolmates and neighbors, went to Church and renewed friendships, sang a solo, and read a reference in the Bible at Young People's meeting. She answered lots of questions asked by old and young. Went to one party and played "Down in Alabam'," "Bow to your Opposite Partner" and many other "floor games" which drew more dust from the carpets than a modern vacuum cleaner.

She had a long and loving talk with her darling mother whose life was given over entirely to duties to her six children and husband. Was teased by her brothers and entered into more than one water fight which usually ended in a free for all. Hung May baskets, got caught and kissed by the big fat red-headed boy who always had a leaky nose—

She returned to Peoria appreciating all the more her good fortune in having her lot cast in this home of Peace and Quiet and Growth.
