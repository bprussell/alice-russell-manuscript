# Chapter XXIII

After a wonderful night's sleep and rest Frances awoke happy and determined to make Parepa spend the happiest day of her life. He would not mention his story or anything that would cause a cloud to cross their path.

The telephone rang—and it was Parepa calling so early. They chatted and laughed awhile and then began to plan the day's pleasure.

"Let's go for a canter this morning," suggested Parepa.

"Fine!" said Frances. "I'll be out with the span we rode the last time. Be there at eight o'clock." When he turned from the 'phone Daddy Wheeler stood facing him.

After greetings he said, "I wonder if it wouldn't be a wise thing for you to avoid mentioning that subject today. It's your last day together and why not make it an entirely happy one? Glad you are going for a ride this nice morning—nothing better for young folks," he added.

Frances was sure Daddy had seen a wakeful night. Why had he worried him about this affair with Parepa? At eight o'clock he and Parepa were in the saddle making the most beautiful picture Aunt Kate and Uncle George had ever seen.

"They are just made for each other," Uncle George was sure of that.

"Parepa is far too old for her age, George. She is as mature in mind and body as a twenty year old. Seems as if she never was a real little girl. The two years she did herself out of when she came here was a crime. I would not have allowed her to carry such a burden of work and study had I any idea she was only fourteen," and Aunt Kate sat down in an easy chair beside her husband.

"Here she is as madly in love with this young fellow as I was at twenty. It's a sin, that's all. Poor little girlie," Aunt Kate wiped a tear from her eye.

"There, there, now mother don't let this thing worry you this way. The kids will get over it. He leaves this burg tomorrow for two years and there's a chance he may never come our way again," Uncle George said trying to comfort her.

"I don't want him to forget her though. I know human nature well enough to know that Parepa will never change. She has found her mate. My only comfort is that she will have these two years to grow into her own," said Mrs. Slay.

"Well, let's not think any more about it anyway. Let's just have some real living these next two years. We have enough saved up so we can afford a vacation while I get my health back," and Mr. Slay lit a cigar in preparation of taking his morning walk.

"But Parepa must go on with her studies and that expensive business. Doctor bills and music lessons soon empty a well filled pocket," she answered.

"Now you are worrying, my dear, and that always makes me feel terrible—to see you worried. Promise me you won't do it anymore," and Mr. Slay kissed his wife kindly. Just as he opened the door to step out Mr. Wheeler stood ready to ring.

"Well, well—come in," said Mr. Slay. Aunt Kate welcomed him.

They talked over the weather and topics of the day for some time. Finally Mr. Wheeler launched on the subject of his errand.

"I suppose Frances has told you we are leaving tomorrow," he said.

"Yes, sorry to see you go," said Uncle George.

"Well, after a few years we won't be so tied down and can take our time. Just now I am anxious to see Frances finish his education," and Mr. Wheeler leaned forward in his chair. "The child is eager to learn all there is to know about life facts. He's a regular diver after such knowledge. It's his hobby. I would venture to say that (child though he seems) he is better posted on sociology than many of your college professors. I encourage him in it for I feel as he does, that the greatest mysteries of life are still to be solved.

"I came over this morning to talk with you about the strong attraction our youngsters have for one another. I feel sure I'm not misplacing my confidence when I tell you some things that have never passed my lips before. Frances was born under unusual circumstances. My father was Frances's mother's guardian. I was thirty years old when she died and she was twenty. I loved this charge of my father's with all my heart but on account of the difference in our ages I did not force my attentions upon her though I was most certain my love was returned. Sometimes I was sure she wanted me to declare my love. On account of this strong attraction I left for Germany where I studied music under the best masters for two years. My heart was not in it and I did not do well. During this time she was in a finishing college. Another man fell in love with her and she imagined herself in love with him. They sinned as had her foremothers. Two weeks before they were to have been married her lover died. This was eight months before Frances's birth.

"When the girl realized her condition she began to live only for the little life within her. My father (and mother, until her death) had very carefully guarded this child from babyhood. Retaining the nurse that assisted the physician in bringing her into this world to care for her physical needs up to her college days. Now that he realized her condition he secured again the services of her old nurse who loved her as her own. To this woman Anna Bell Emmerson confided her every thought. They were all for her baby. She wrote her wishes clearly and definitely. There could be no mistaking them. She knew for sure as if she had some special intimacy with the Gods of fate that she too could not survive the ordeal of childbirth. This did not seem to worry her. She seemed to feel it a fact not to be wrestled with and allowed no efforts made to correct the heritage that was hers. Often she would write for hours messages to her unborn babe. Messages to be opened at stated ages of life. She went so far as to send for me to come home. It was her desire I be her child's guardian.

"She addressed long letters with requests to be fulfilled to both her nurse and myself. She wanted her nurse to stay with her baby as she had with her, etc. Her mother had fallen heir to the family locket of huge size strung on a thin chain of gold. This in turn became the possession of Anna Bell. It was her idea to place certain truth requests, advice, secrets and wishes together with her picture into this locket, take it to a jeweler and witness its sealing with a band of gold on which was this inscription: 'To be opened on the fourteenth birthday of my darling child,' signed Anna Bell Emmerson.

"She urged her nurse and I to see that this locket was kept near her baby at all times until it was old enough to wear it in safety. He was to be given this locket to play with at times and made to know it was a most precious article. After his fourteenth birthday he was to wear it continually through life as a part of himself.

"As the time drew near my father learned of a wonderful physician. A specialist in Glasgow that was highly recommended as being one surgeon that could bring a child into the world safely and save the mother even though conditions were mightily against her.

"Anna Bell, my father, the nurse, myself, and two of America's best physicians sailed for Glasgow on August 12th.

"On August 15th while on the ship she sent for me and told me as calmly as you and I are talking of her love for me. How she had craved my love as we roamed the gardens, rode together over the estate, read together and played together. She told me how she had hoped I'd give up the big brother idea and grab her in my arms and cover her with real love kisses. It nearly killed me. I hardly heard her as she said quietly, 'Kiss me. Then call the Doctors and nurse now, my baby, *our* baby, yours and mine wants to see the world. I give it to you, wrapped in its flesh and blood is all my love. Take it and keep it forever.'

"I scarcely remember another thing until two days later, August 17th, twenty years ago a beautiful baby—my baby—was brought to me and placed in my arms. The letter which was to be given me with it lay on its blanket.

"'Shall I leave you alone?' the nurse asked.

"'Yes,' I answered. Oh, the miserable joy I felt in the warmth of that little human. I opened the letter and read its message for I knew the mother's life had fled even though no one told me. I can never repeat what I read as I scanned the most astounding request ever made by a mother. *It Shall be*—*it shall be*! I cried as I paced the floor with this, my newborn son in my arms."
