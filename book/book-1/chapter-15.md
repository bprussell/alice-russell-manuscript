# Chapter XV

That night Parepa could not sleep. The first time in her life she could remember of laying awake, no matter how troubled the day had been. She didn't know just why she couldn't sleep—all she knew was that she was happy, Oh, so happy.

She heard nothing from Frances for several days. She caught herself watching the street hoping he would pass and maybe—stop in.

He didn't—but one day she was called to the 'phone. It was Frances. "Just got back in town," he said. "What you going to do tonight?"

She told him it was choir rehearsal tonight.

"Can I go, too?" he begged.

"Surely you can—glad to have you. Come out early," she said.

"Alright—'bout seven—bye, bye."

"Bye," and she hung up so flushed and smiling that the other girls teased her.

"I'm going to tell Mr. Slay," said Carrie and she pointed an accusing finger at Parepa.

That night Aunt Kate begged off and stayed home from practice.

"I always go early to rehearse my solo before the others get there," she told him as they strolled toward the church.

"You don't mean to say you are singing solos already?" He stopped still and looked at her. She was only a child, sweet and innocent as a rose and so talented. She was a picture to him—a perfect picture. The picture of youth and life and—love.

When he sat in a side seat and listened to her sing "The Beautiful Gates Ajar" he wondered why she practiced. That song was perfect. No human being could improve upon it.

He was introduced to Professor Blough again and asked to join in the singing. This he gladly did and it was Parepa's turn to be surprised. Oh, what a voice! Why hadn't he told her of his wonderful progress? Then she remembered she had never told him of her success either. On the way home they talked of the love for music, dancing, art, flowers, literature, and sports.

"We seem to like the same things in the same way," observed Frances.

"Yes, isn't it nice to be like that? We can have great times together I know. The parks are so beautiful now. Let's spend lots of time there this summer," she suggested.

"Fine!—but you will be working the best part of the day—the morning," he answered. "I hate that old candy shop. It's no place for a girl like you with your possibilities. Why don't you cut it out and spend your time on the things becoming to you?"

"You forget, Frances, that it is from the candy shop my 'possibilities' as you call them are made possible. I'll always have a warm spot in my heart for 313 Main Street, Peoria, Illinois, no matter how far from it I roam."

"Yes, I suppose so but it shouldn't be necessary for you to stay there any longer. You should enter a good finishing school this fall," he said emphatically.

"Finishing school!" and Parepa laughed aloud. "Finishing school, why I've never entered a beginners' school yet. You forget the only schooling I ever had was what I got in a little old one room schoolhouse a mile across the field from our house. The last day I went to school I went barefooted, wore a pink gingham dress, and a big sunbonnet."

"Wish I'd happened along about that time, Goldie Locks," he teased. "But seriously it's time you gave all your attention to your talents."

"I have the whole month of August off duty at the shop and am not going to study one thing during that month. Won't that seem funny? Just going to play and play all month," she laughed.

"Yes, and I know someone that's going to play and play with you, too. That is, if you will let me," and he squeezed her hand tight.

"Alright, what will we play? Let's plan it all out. I love to ride horseback and swim and row a boat and play tennis and just stroll and stroll and stroll around in beautiful Glen Oak Park. I love everything beautiful, Frances, and I can't control my passionate love for God's flowers. Wasn't it grand of him to put so many of them in the world for us to enjoy? I'm just possessed by their beauty and fragrance."

"I love beautiful things, too, but most of all beautiful lives—like yours, you know. I'm not surprised that you love all these things. It's just what I'd expect of you."
