# Chapter XIII

In these two years letters came from and went to Frances at regular intervals. Parepa always read them and deposited them in her "keepsake drawer" as she called it, never once offering them to Aunt Kate to read. Mrs. Slay was amused by this little trick in her niece, that was so unlike her, but was satisfied to hear only what Parepa was willing to divulge.

She knew from the size of the incoming and outgoing letters that there couldn't have been much left unsaid. Parepa would always say, "He sends you both his love," and Uncle George would tease, "And what did he send you?" A witty answer was always on her tongue end.

One June day the postmark was unmistakably Halifax, Nova Scotia.

"He's getting nearer, I see," said Uncle George as he handed her the letter.

"Oh, goody, goody, aren't we glad?"

"Bet we are," said Aunt Kate, "unless he has changed a lot."

"Oh, he hasn't one bit I know," and Parepa disappeared into her bedroom as was her custom when she had an unopened letter in her hand.

Uncle George and Aunt Kate fell to reviewing the changes Frances might see in Parepa.

"She is much more beautiful, taller, more stately, can beat him I'm sure in tennis, can swim, a splendid dancer, is beautiful in the saddle, and can enjoy all the auto rides he's able to furnish. Won't he be surprised to find her soloist in the church he attended the last Sunday he was here and giving whole evening dramatic entertainments? Also to find her still working in the 'candy shop,'" said Uncle George.

"We should show him we did not need any of his money to educate her."

"Wonder if she has told him of the many beaux she has had," he chuckled.

"None of them lasted long enough to call them beaux," corrected Aunt Kate. "I just wonder if he has changed as much?"

Parepa coming down the stairs laughed and said, "He sends both of you his love. Ha! Ha!"

"We want to know more than that. When's he going to get here?" asked Uncle George.

"Don't know yet. Sometime though, Mr. Wheeler is coming with him. They have business that will detain them indefinitely where they are," and she set about helping with the supper, singing as she worked. She has proved herself a musical genius as well as a born actress. The pride that swelled the hearts of these two older people to almost bursting was excusable for to them was due all the credit for "opening this chestnut burr."

The time was now at hand when Parepa must go forward. A higher course in music and dramatic art must be arranged for her before the fall terms opened. What could be done about it—we'll see.

The next two weeks were weeks of anxious waiting at the Slay home. Not only was Parepa all a flutter but Uncle George and Aunt Kate were quite as excited.

"I suppose they will stay at the best hotel in town this time," ventured Mrs. Slay as she and her husband were discussing plans for their amusement during their stay.

"Yes, and anyway why should we be worrying over what they shall do for entertainment? Don't think we'll be included in their social activities anyway. Let's wait and see how they act."
