# Chapter III

"Put on your best dress for we are going downtown to shop today," and Aunt Kate led the way to the bathroom where she carefully dressed the delighted girl. She was as pink and white as a lily and her eyes were dancing with anticipation. She had always wanted to see the pretty things in the big stores. Aunt Kate had her breakfast all fixed on the kitchen table. She was not long eating it and was soon waiting with her aunt on the corner for a streetcar. She enjoyed the ride downtown and was sorry when it ended.

First, they went to a shoe store. Aunt Kate explained to the clerk the kind of "growing girl" shoe she wanted and Parepa-Roza was ushered to a seat to be fitted. She whispered to Aunt Kate that she only had fifty cents in her pocketbook but Aunt Kate told her that she and Uncle George had laid awake last night and planned to play that she was their own Maude and to get her the things they would like to have gotten for Maude was she living.

"She will just wear them," she said to the clerk as the pair she liked best had been fitted. They were pretty and well-fitted so Parepa was much more comfortable than she had been before.

Next they looked at hats. A broad brimmed leghorn with a wreath of daisies and roses trailing off the edge of the brim and hanging down the back was chosen. Also, a blue and white tam o'shanter. Parepa was so delighted when she looked at herself in the long mirror.

"She'll just wear it," said Aunt Kate and left the old cheap faded hat to be delivered. "Now let's get some new underwear," and Aunt Kate steered her into the largest store yet.

Parepa saw so many things that captivated her she could hardly keep up with her auntie. Stockings were the first things; three pair of black ones and one of white, three petticoats, three suits of underwear and then on to look at dresses. Such a pretty pink dotted swiss was chosen first, then a lace trimmed, tucked and ruffled white organdy and last a blue sailor dress with white braid on the collar, cuffs and belt. Also a red, white and blue emblem on one sleeve. A pretty checked gingham seemed necessary, too.

"Now for the hair ribbons," said Aunt Kate. "We will only get three today." Aunt Kate loved to "fool" with this girl's lovely wavy hair and tied a bow of ribbon to match her dress about halfway down her braid always making a few curls below the bow.

"It's most one o'clock," said Aunt Kate, "and I guess we'll just get a lunch downtown." They went into Densburger's on Adams St. and Parepa opened her eyes in wonder as she watched Aunt Kate deliberately order the good things that young girls like to eat. She also took note as to the way to handle her food and did credit to the more sophisticated. Aunt Kate noticed how quickly the child "caught on" to the way of things and had great hopes for her future progress.

"Now we'll order the things for the picnic supper tonight, and then go home and rest up awhile," she said.

When they arrived home Uncle George welcomed them on the front porch. "Say, what's under that hat," he laughed as he raised one drooping side, "and new shoes! And of all the boxes and bundles that have been delivered here. Let's see what's in them." Uncle George had been so happy helping to plan for this buying expedition and could hardly wait to see her "dressed up" in her new things.

What fun it was! Parepa paraded in front of him in each outfit. As she did this she wished inwardly that all the folks at home as well as her schoolmates could see her now. Only a day since she left home and yet how many things had happened.

"They'll do," said Uncle George as he smiled his pleasure at Aunt Kate's taste. "Wish John and Eliza could see her now."

"Now we'll lie down a while and rest up for tonight," said Aunt Kate as she took off the last dress over the girl's head and carefully placed it on a hanger in Parepa's closet. "I'll call you in time, see if you can't sleep a little." Parepa laughed at the idea. She laid on her bed perhaps ten full minutes before sleep claimed her. She did not realize that her young body had been greatly taxed by the "goings on" of the last forty hours. Wise Aunt Kate knew a rest was a necessity. She was anxious that Parepa be at her best that evening for the picnic and tennis lessons. Instead of going to bed herself she slipped down the stairs to the kitchen where she and Uncle George prepared the lunch for the picnic supper.

As the work progressed they talked continually of Parepa and her future. How they would go about it to convenience John and Eliza that they should let them have her. They even planned how her music lessons and schoolwork could be managed and Kate declared she felt like the child belonged to her already.
