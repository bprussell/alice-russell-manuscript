# Chapter V

The next day was Sunday and Parepa-Roza asked what Church and Sunday School they went to.

"Well, Missie, we haven't gone to Church for a long time, but we'll go wherever you want to," said Uncle George. "There's plenty of them near here. Let's see, there's an Episcopal, Baptist, Universalist, Congregational, Methodist, Catholic, Christian Lutheran, and Presbyterian all within walking distance. Take your choice," he said smiling.

"Let's go to the Congregational then," and that was settled once and for all for Auntie and Uncle found themselves sitting up straight in a pew every Sunday morning and found themselves really enjoying the sermons and music.

"Isn't it queer how we ever got out of the habit of going to Church?" said Aunt Kate one Sunday after enjoying an especially interesting sermon.

"Guess we just quit and never had anyone to start us in again until our girl here came our way," returned Uncle George and he took Parepa's chin in his hand and turned her face to his. "Do you know you have a great responsibility in this family, my dear, keeping your old Auntie and Uncle going straight."

Parepa only laughed. This was one of her charms, but she didn't realize it. When she laughed she displayed a beautiful row of pearly teeth, her cheeks dimpled, her eyes flashed, while the laugh itself was a musical ripple which captivated its hearers. When she didn't know what to say, as in this instance, her laugh came to the rescue and was all that was necessary.

Parepa had been in the City over a week now and tomorrow, Oh Happy Day! she was to start working. She had visited the other relatives in Peoria with Uncle George as pilot but he insisted she must return home with him on every occasion. "John trusted her with us and we are going to take good care of her," he would tell them.

The fact was, he was very proud of his new responsibility and a little jealous if anyone showed signs of wanting to relieve him of it. He warned Uncle Will that she was only working for him and that *he*, himself, was really her guardian.
