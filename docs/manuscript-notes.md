# Manuscript Notes

Facts about the manuscript itself, gathered during transcription. This feeds
the foreword / "about this edition" material in Phase 4.

## The author and the submission

Every page carries a typed submission header:

> Submitted by:
> Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
> Mt. Hawley Road
> Edelstein, Illinois

Page 1 also carries a typed length declaration at the right margin —
**"100,000 words"** — the standard format for a manuscript submitted to a
publisher. She wasn't just typing this for the family drawer; she was
submitting it for publication. (100,000 words across ~289 pages ≈ 346
words/page, which is consistent with the page density observed so far.)

## The title

Page 1 carries the book's title, erased but partially recoverable. Contrast
enhancement of the two available photos of page 1 shows a centered
three-line stack between the submission header and "Chaper I":

```
[first line — unreadable in current photos]
                 OF
        [word(s)?] SECRETS
```

The middle line reads **"OF"** with high confidence; the last line ends in
**"SECRETS"** with high confidence (matching the family's partial memory of
"… or … secret" — the "or" was evidently "OF"). So the title has the shape
"_____ OF _____ SECRETS" or "_____ / OF / SECRETS". The first line resists
both photos at maximum enhancement.

**Recovery options for the physical page:**

1. **Raking light photo.** In a dark room, hold a flashlight nearly flat
   against the page surface, shining across the erased area from the side,
   and photograph straight down, close up. Typewriter keys dent the paper;
   erasing removes ink but not the dents, and shallow side-light throws them
   into shadow. Try light from two or three directions.
2. **The back of the page.** The same dents emboss through; a raking-light
   photo of the verso (which will read mirror-image) sometimes shows erased
   lines more clearly than the front.
3. **Look for the title elsewhere.** Boxes of submitted manuscripts often
   held a separate title page or cover letter, and authors sometimes typed
   the title on the last page or on the box lid.

## The story (as of pages 1–2)

Chapter I opens on **Alice Parepa-Roza**, a tawny-haired, freckle-faced,
ten-year-old daydreamer in the house of the stern **John Wesley**, scolded
for lying in the pasture past dark watching cloud-shapes (castles, cattle
drives, battles). The family: six children, a farm, Saturday trading trips to
**Chillicothe** — a real town near Edelstein in Peoria County, Illinois, so
the story is set in the author's own countryside. Given that the heroine
shares the author's first name, this may be autobiographical fiction.

(The heroine's unusual name echoes Euphrosyne Parepa-Rosa, a famous
19th-century opera soprano whose troupe toured the American Midwest — a
plausible namesake a musical family might have chosen. Speculation only.)
