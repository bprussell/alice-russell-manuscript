# Manuscript Notes

Facts about the manuscript itself, gathered during transcription. This feeds
the foreword / "about this edition" material in Phase 4.

## The author and the submission

Every page carries a typed submission header:

> Submitted by:
> Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
> Mt. Hawley Road
> Edelstein, Illinois

Page 1 also carries a typed length declaration at the right margin —
**"100,000 words"** — the standard format for a manuscript submitted to a
publisher. She wasn't just typing this for the family drawer; she was
submitting it for publication.

**Actual length (counted 2026-10-11, all pages transcribed):** about
**82,000 words** as typed (81,953 across the 288 verbatim transcripts,
≈285 words/page), and 81,005 in the edited reading text — Book I 45,850,
Book II 35,153. Her "100,000" was a round-number estimate about 20% high,
typical of the era's estimate-by-page method; the declaration sits on
Book I's first page but evidently covers both books.

## The author's family

Source: "the Russell book" (a family history; chapter 16, p. 82, entry
R287, "John Sidney Russell 1879–1961 / Alice Parapa-Rosa Reynolds
1881–1975"; photo in docs/russell-book-bio.heic), plus Robert F.
Russell's obituary (Peoria *Journal Star*, 10 Dec 1998) and public
burial lists.

- **Alice Parepa-Rosa Reynolds, 1881–1975**, daughter of **John and Eliza
  (Bonham) Reynolds**. Called "Allie." Taught at the **Kellogg school**,
  where she met her husband.
- **John Sidney Russell, 24 May 1879 – 1961**, seventh child of John and
  Sarah (Horn) Russell, born on the homestead farm; schooled at the
  Kellogg school. Married Alice **21 June 1905**.
- Farmed after marriage; when his health failed they bought the **General
  Store in Speer** (south side of Main Street, next to the hotel) and ran
  it a few years. In 1912 back to farming and livestock; he also
  delivered milk on an Edelstein route. In 1926 moved to a farm just
  north of Edelstein, known as "Russellville," with an airplane beacon
  light on one corner.
- Sons: **Robert Francis** (b. 14 Jun 1910, Speer; d. 9 Dec 1998, Peoria),
  **Richard LaVerne** (b. 1913), **Arthur Emerson** (b. 1915; very likely
  the Arthur Russell of Chillicothe d. 1 Jan 2001, buried Lawn Ridge
  Cemetery). All attended Centerville grade school and Princeville High
  School.
- Later: the Hodge farm (Valley Township, Stark County); John worked for
  the Bemis Bag Co. in Peoria; then Peoria, first Mt. Hawley Rd. (now
  Knoxville Ave.), then 1218 N. Sheridan, where Allie ran a flower
  business, "The Russell Flower Garden"; she lectured on flowers and her
  button collection; matron at Caterpillar; member of Edelstein
  Congregational Church, Toastmistress Club, Little Garden Club, Retired
  Teachers Association. John d. 13 Oct 1961 (82); Allie d. 5 May 1975
  (93); both buried Swan Lake Memorial Gardens, Peoria.
- A newspaper clipping credits her with five books: "Sealed Secrets,"
  "Kshema," "'Tis Our God Rules Over All," "Then What," and "Button,
  Button, Who Gave the Button?" Only this manuscript is known to survive.
- Her middle name: the family history spells it "Parapa-Rosa" and says
  she claimed it was that of a famous Italian opera singer, whose name her
  father liked (the novel tells this too). The singer was Euphrosyne
  Parepa-Rosa (1836–1874), so "Parapa" is taken as a misspelling; this
  edition uses **Parepa-Rosa** for the author. (Her heroine is spelled
  "Parepa-Roza" throughout the typescript, and is left that way.) No
  primary record of the middle name has been found.
- The manuscript was kept by her grandson **William Russell, Richard's
  son**.

**How closely the novel follows her life:** the heroine is Alice
Parepa-Roza — the author's own name; Parepa's parents are "John and
Eliza" (her parents' real names); Allie teaches at "the old Kellogg
schoolhouse" and marries Johnie, son of a Scotch family, alias "J.S.
Bradford" (John Sidney); Johnie's health fails; they run a store; her
first son Robert is born "on June the fourteenth, 1910"; her sons are
Robert, Richard and Arthur. And the sons' real middle names — Robert
**Francis**, Richard **LaVerne**, Arthur **Emerson** — together spell the
name of the novel's first love, **Frances LaVerne Emmerson**.

## The title

Page 1 carries the book's title, erased but partially recoverable. Contrast
enhancement of the two available photos of page 1 shows a centered
three-line stack between the submission header and "Chaper I":

```
[first line — unreadable in current photos]
                 OF
        [word(s)?] SECRETS
```

The middle line reads **"OF"** with high confidence; the last line ends in
**"SECRETS"** with high confidence (matching the family's partial memory of
"… or … secret" — the "or" was evidently "OF"). So the title has the shape
"_____ OF _____ SECRETS" or "_____ / OF / SECRETS". The first line resists
both photos at maximum enhancement.

**Recovery outcome (2026-07-08/09):** while the physical manuscript was
accessible, the family attempted recovery of Book I's title — reading the
erased block from the back of the sheet and at all angles — without
success; the physical pages are no longer accessible, so Book I's title
is permanently unrecoverable beyond the "_____ OF _____ SECRETS" shape
(ghost-line width suggests a ~5-7 letter word preceded "SECRETS").

**Book II's title, however, was substantially recovered.** Its erased
block on page 165 shares the same three-line "X / OF / Y" shape, and a
family reading of the ghost impressions recovered
"____IR / __ / TH_ BR____ C__IN" — the last line reading **"THE BROKEN
CHAIN"** letter-for-letter, which is the manuscript's own closing image
("The last link in the Emmerson chain was broken.") and the fulfillment
of page 162's vow ("He shall break this line of weakness and shame").
The first line's "...IR" fits **"THE HEIR"** — and Allie is Emmerson's
heir in the plot. Probable original: **"THE HEIR OF THE BROKEN CHAIN"**.

**Titles for the reading edition (revised 2026-11; see book/edits.md):**
the Russell family history (p. 84) records the two parts as **"Sealed
Secrets"** and **"Kashema"** — the titles used. "SEALED" fits the ~6-letter
gap before "SECRETS" on page 1, so the erased Book I block probably read
"[?] / OF / SEALED SECRETS" or similar; "The Heir of the Broken Chain" was
evidently an earlier title for Book II, later replaced by "Kashema" (the
name of the house in the story). The earlier composed title "The Locket of
Family Secrets" is retired.

## The story (as of pages 1–2)

Chapter I opens on **Alice Parepa-Roza**, a tawny-haired, freckle-faced,
ten-year-old daydreamer in the house of the stern **John Wesley**, scolded
for lying in the pasture past dark watching cloud-shapes (castles, cattle
drives, battles). The family: six children, a farm, Saturday trading trips to
**Chillicothe** — a real town near Edelstein in Peoria County, Illinois, so
the story is set in the author's own countryside. Given that the heroine
shares the author's first name, this may be autobiographical fiction.

(The heroine's unusual name is the author's own middle name, after the
opera soprano Euphrosyne Parepa-Rosa; the family history confirms she
claimed the singer as its source.)

## Completeness and page numbering

The full manuscript was photographed in a single session (289 photos: one
keepsake shot of the manuscript in its box, then 288 page photos in
capture order). The final page ends with an Omar Khayyám quatrain ("Ah,
love, could you and I with fate conspire…"), the line "The last link in the
Emmerson chain was broken. They had taken their long ride." and a centered
**THE END** — the book is complete.

**The manuscript is two books in one volume, tête-bêche style.** Book I
ends on physical page 164 (typed page 162) with a centered typed
instruction:

> TURN THIS BOOK UPSIDE DOWN AND BEGIN ON FIRST PAGE

Book II then begins at physical page 165 with its own Chapter I and fresh
page numbering 1–124 (confirmed: physical 189 = II:25, physical 288 =
II:124 — the arithmetic checks exactly). Book I runs two pages ahead of
its typed numbers (physical 164 = typed 162), consistent with the
unnumbered first page and one other unnumbered page — Phase 1's per-page
records will pin down which.

Because Book II was physically oriented upside-down relative to Book I
(that's the point of the turn-it-over instruction), many page photos came
out inverted; all 288 pages were individually orientation-verified and
corrected during ingest. **Open question for the family:** is Book II
typed on the backs of Book I's sheets, or on separate sheets stacked
behind? (Look at the back of any Book I page.) Either answer is a nice
detail for the foreword.

Book I ends with Frances's farewell ("I shall never love another woman in
my life") and the two young people "making a new start in life. Each going
their separate ways; for better or for worse." Book II opens back on the
farm with Allie and follows the family's later years.
