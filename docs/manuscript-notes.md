# Manuscript Notes

Facts about the manuscript itself, gathered during transcription. This feeds
the foreword / "about this edition" material in Phase 4.

## The author and the submission

Every page carries a typed submission header:

> Submitted by:
> Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell
> Mt. Hawley Road
> Edelstein, Illinois

Page 1 also carries a typed length declaration at the right margin —
**"100,000 words"** — the standard format for a manuscript submitted to a
publisher. She wasn't just typing this for the family drawer; she was
submitting it for publication. (100,000 words across 288 pages ≈ 347
words/page, which is consistent with the page density observed so far.)

## The title

Page 1 carries the book's title, erased but partially recoverable. Contrast
enhancement of the two available photos of page 1 shows a centered
three-line stack between the submission header and "Chaper I":

```
[first line — unreadable in current photos]
                 OF
        [word(s)?] SECRETS
```

The middle line reads **"OF"** with high confidence; the last line ends in
**"SECRETS"** with high confidence (matching the family's partial memory of
"… or … secret" — the "or" was evidently "OF"). So the title has the shape
"_____ OF _____ SECRETS" or "_____ / OF / SECRETS". The first line resists
both photos at maximum enhancement.

**Recovery outcome (2026-07-08):** while the physical manuscript was
accessible, the family attempted recovery — reading the erased block from
the back of the sheet and at all angles — without success, and the
physical pages are no longer accessible, so the title is permanently
unrecoverable beyond the "_____ OF _____ SECRETS" shape. (Book II's
opening page, 165, carries a similar pair of erased/ghost title lines,
equally unrecoverable.)

**Editorial decision for the reading edition:** the reading edition will
carry editor-composed titles that fit the books, and the foreword will
explain that the original titles were erased and that these invented
titles are the edition's one truly editorial contribution. Recorded in
book/edits.md.

## The story (as of pages 1–2)

Chapter I opens on **Alice Parepa-Roza**, a tawny-haired, freckle-faced,
ten-year-old daydreamer in the house of the stern **John Wesley**, scolded
for lying in the pasture past dark watching cloud-shapes (castles, cattle
drives, battles). The family: six children, a farm, Saturday trading trips to
**Chillicothe** — a real town near Edelstein in Peoria County, Illinois, so
the story is set in the author's own countryside. Given that the heroine
shares the author's first name, this may be autobiographical fiction.

(The heroine's unusual name echoes Euphrosyne Parepa-Rosa, a famous
19th-century opera soprano whose troupe toured the American Midwest — a
plausible namesake a musical family might have chosen. Speculation only.)

## Completeness and page numbering

The full manuscript was photographed in a single session (289 photos: one
keepsake shot of the manuscript in its box, then 288 page photos in
capture order). The final page ends with an Omar Khayyám quatrain ("Ah,
love, could you and I with fate conspire…"), the line "The last link in the
Emmerson chain was broken. They had taken their long ride." and a centered
**THE END** — the book is complete.

**The manuscript is two books in one volume, tête-bêche style.** Book I
ends on physical page 164 (typed page 162) with a centered typed
instruction:

> TURN THIS BOOK UPSIDE DOWN AND BEGIN ON FIRST PAGE

Book II then begins at physical page 165 with its own Chapter I and fresh
page numbering 1–124 (confirmed: physical 189 = II:25, physical 288 =
II:124 — the arithmetic checks exactly). Book I runs two pages ahead of
its typed numbers (physical 164 = typed 162), consistent with the
unnumbered first page and one other unnumbered page — Phase 1's per-page
records will pin down which.

Because Book II was physically oriented upside-down relative to Book I
(that's the point of the turn-it-over instruction), many page photos came
out inverted; all 288 pages were individually orientation-verified and
corrected during ingest. **Open question for the family:** is Book II
typed on the backs of Book I's sheets, or on separate sheets stacked
behind? (Look at the back of any Book I page.) Either answer is a nice
detail for the foreword.

Book I ends with Frances's farewell ("I shall never love another woman in
my life") and the two young people "making a new start in life. Each going
their separate ways; for better or for worse." Book II opens back on the
farm with Allie and follows the family's later years.
