## The verse

Four times in this book a character reaches for the same four lines:
Parepa, to comfort Frances on the night Frances's secret comes out; Allie,
sitting beside Johnie's sickbed, and again at the supper table with
bankruptcy at the door; and the author herself, on the last page, just
before THE END.

The lines are from the *Rubáiyát of Omar Khayyám*, the quatrains of a
Persian poet and astronomer who lived some nine hundred years ago, in the
English version Edward FitzGerald published in 1859. FitzGerald's little
book sold almost nothing at first, but by the time Alice was a young
woman it had become one of the most quoted poems in America, printed in
gift editions and recited in parlors, and its verses were the kind people
carried in their heads.

That is evidently how she carried this one. She quotes it the same way
each time:

> Ah, love, could you and I with fate conspire,  
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To grasp this sorry scheme of things entire;  
> Would we not shatter it to bits and then,  
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Remould it nearer to the heart's desire?

FitzGerald's first edition reads "could *thou* and I with Fate
conspire," and his final edition, the one most often reprinted, "could
you and I with *Him* conspire"; both have "Would not we shatter it to
bits." Hers falls between the two: the words as she remembered them, and
as her characters, who also quote it from memory, would have said them.
This edition keeps her wording, and sets it the same way all four times.

## The titles

Above the first chapter of each book she once typed a title in a centered
block, and later erased it. Of Book One's, only "OF" and "SECRETS" can
still be made out, with room before "SECRETS" for a word of about six
letters, just the space "SEALED" would fill.

Book Two's could be read from the impression the type left in the paper:
*The Heir of the Broken Chain*. It is the image she ended on. Frances was
to be the one who would "break this line of weakness and shame," and the
book's last words before the verse are "The last link in the Emmerson
chain was broken. They had taken their long ride." Parepa is the heir.

Sometime afterward she settled on the titles her family remembers.
*Kashema* is the name Frances gave the house in the desert, "meaning
'Home, a place of rest, repose, security, and peace.'"
