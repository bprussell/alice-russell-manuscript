The book you are holding was written by Alice Reynolds Russell
(1881–1975). For most of a century it existed as a single typewritten
manuscript: 288 loose pages, toned to a dark cream, kept in a box by her
grandson, William (Bill) Russell. This edition is the first time it has been set
in type.

She was born Alice Parepa-Rosa Reynolds, the daughter of John and Eliza
(Bonham) Reynolds. As a young woman she taught at the Kellogg school, where
she met John Sidney Russell, a farmer's son who had gone to school there
himself; they were married on 21 June 1905. When Johnnie's health failed
they kept the general store in Speer for a few years, then went back to
the land, farming, raising livestock, and delivering milk on an Edelstein
route. Their sons were Robert (1910), Richard (1913), and Arthur (1915).
In 1926 they moved to a farm just north of Edelstein that the family
called "Russellville," where the beam of an airplane beacon on one corner
swept through the bedrooms at night. Later came the Hodge farm, just over
the line in Stark County, while John worked for the Bemis Bag Company in
Peoria, and then Peoria itself: a house on Mt. Hawley Road, and finally
1218 North Sheridan.

The family history remembers her as "quite a unique person," a stylish
lady, and "definitely the one in the extended family who reached for and
wanted ‘greater things.’" She lectured to groups throughout central
Illinois on her large collection of buttons. She was for many years a
matron at Caterpillar, and a member of the Edelstein Congregational
Church, the Toastmistress Club, the Little Garden Club, and the Retired
Teachers Association. Johnnie died at their home on Sheridan on 13
October 1961, at 82; Allie lived on there until her death on 5 May 1975,
at 93. They are buried at Swan Lake Memorial Gardens in Peoria.

A newspaper clipping says she wrote five books: *Sealed Secrets*,
*Kashema*, *’Tis Our God Rules Over All*, *Then What*, and *Button,
Button, Who Gave the Button?*, the last a history of her button
collection. Only the first two are known to survive, together, in the
manuscript printed here. When her son Robert shared a copy with the
wider family, they called it "an autobiography that glides freely between
the factual and the fanciful. You are never just sure which is which."
Readers who knew her will find her everywhere in it, beginning with her
heroine's name.

She did not write it only for the family drawer. Every page carries a
typed heading, *Submitted by: Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell, Mt. Hawley
Road, Edelstein, Illinois*, and the first page bears the declaration
"100,000 words" at its margin, the standard form for a manuscript sent to
a publisher. (A careful count puts the true length nearer 82,000 words;
the round figure was the customary estimate of her day.) Whether it was
ever sent, and what answer came back, we do not know.

## Two books in one

The manuscript is really two novels bound together. The first ends with a
typed instruction at the foot of its last page, TURN THIS BOOK UPSIDE DOWN
AND BEGIN ON FIRST PAGE, and the second begins, with its own Chapter I and
its own page numbers, from the other end. Book One follows a dreamy,
freckle-faced farm girl, Alice Parepa-Roza Wesley, from her father's farm
to her aunt and uncle's home in Peoria. Book Two returns to her years
later. The countryside is the author's own: Chillicothe, Speer, Peoria,
Glen Oak Park, and the farms around Edelstein.

## The titles

The family has always known the two books as *Sealed Secrets* and
*Kashema*, and those are the titles used here. The titles she first typed
on the manuscript were later erased; what can still be read of them is
told in the afterword, since it gives away the ending.

## About this edition

Every page was first transcribed exactly as typed (misspellings,
strikeovers, her pencil corrections in the margins, all of it), and that
word-for-word record is preserved alongside this edition. The reading text
you have here was then lightly edited so that her story, rather than her
typewriter, has your attention:

- typing slips and misspellings are corrected, and her own pencil
  corrections are applied;
- spelling, capitalization, and punctuation follow modern American usage:
  "nevertheless" for her "never-the-less," "the doctor" for "the Doctor";
- a small number of grammatical slips in the narration are set right;
- unspoken thoughts appear in *italics*, and quotation marks are kept for
  spoken words.

Her words, her phrasing, and the voices of her characters are her own.
Where her people speak plainly ("it don't seem possible," "ain't you,
honey?") they speak as she wrote them. Words that were ordinary in her
day and have since grown unfamiliar, such as *cateress*, *bier*, and
*gasolene*, are left as she used them. Every change made between her pages
and these is recorded, so that nothing she wrote is lost. Photographs of
all 288 original pages, the word-for-word transcription, and the full log
of edits are kept together online at
github.com/bprussell/alice-russell-manuscript.

This edition was prepared in 2026 by her great-grandson Brad Russell
for his father, Bill, and Bill's siblings, and for all of Alice's
family, so that her book might at last be read as she meant it to be.
