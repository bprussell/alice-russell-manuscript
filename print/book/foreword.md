The book you are holding was written by Alice Reynolds Russell
(1881–1975) of Edelstein, Illinois. For most of a century it existed as a
single typewritten manuscript: 288 loose pages, toned to a dark cream,
kept in a box by her grandson, William Russell. This edition is the first
time it has been set in type.

She was born Alice Parapa-Rosa Reynolds, the daughter of John and Eliza
(Bonham) Reynolds. As a young woman she taught at the Kellogg school,
where she met John Sidney Russell, a farmer's son who had gone to school
there himself; they were married on 21 June 1905. When Johnnie's health
failed they sold the farm and kept the general store in Speer for a few
years, then went back to the land, farming, raising livestock, and
delivering milk on an Edelstein route. Their sons were Robert (1910),
Richard (1913), and Arthur (1915). In 1926 they moved to the farm just
north of Edelstein that the family called "Russellville," where the beam
of an airplane beacon on one corner swept through the bedrooms at night.
John died in 1961; Alice lived to 1975.

She did not write it only for the family drawer. Every page carries a typed
heading — *Submitted by: Mrs Alice Reynolds Russell, Mt. Hawley Road,
Edelstein, Illinois* — and the first page bears the declaration
"100,000 words" at its margin, the standard form for a manuscript sent to a
publisher. (A careful count puts the true length nearer 82,000 words; the
round figure was the customary estimate of her day.) Whether it was ever
sent, and what answer came back, we do not know.

## Two books in one

The manuscript is really two novels bound together. The first ends with a
typed instruction at the foot of its last page — TURN THIS BOOK UPSIDE DOWN
AND BEGIN ON FIRST PAGE — and the second begins, with its own Chapter I and
its own page numbers, from the other end. Book One follows a dreamy,
freckle-faced farm girl, Alice Parepa-Roza Wesley, from her father's farm to
her aunt and uncle's home in Peoria. Book Two returns to her years later.
The countryside is the author's own: Chillicothe, Peoria, Glen Oak Park, and
the farms around Edelstein. Readers who knew her may notice that her
heroine shares her first name.

## The titles

Both books once had titles, typed in a centered block above the first
chapter, and both were later erased — by the author herself, it seems. Book
Two's could be partly read from the impression the type left in the paper:
the letters "____IR / __ / TH_ BR____ C__IN" spell out *The Heir of the
Broken Chain*, a phrase that matches the book's own closing image. Book
One's resisted every attempt; only "OF" and "SECRETS" survive. The title
used here, *The Locket of Family Secrets*, was composed by the family
around those two words. It is the one thing in this book that she did not
write.

## About this edition

Every page was first transcribed exactly as typed — misspellings,
strikeovers, her pencil corrections in the margins, all of it — and that
word-for-word record is preserved alongside this edition. The reading text
you have here was then lightly edited so that her story, rather than her
typewriter, has your attention:

- typing slips and misspellings are corrected, and her own pencil
  corrections are applied;
- spelling, capitalization, and punctuation follow modern American usage —
  "nevertheless" for her "never-the-less," "the doctor" for "the Doctor";
- a small number of grammatical slips in the narration are set right;
- unspoken thoughts appear in *italics*, and quotation marks are kept for
  spoken words.

Her words, her phrasing, and the voices of her characters are her own.
Where her people speak plainly — "it don't seem possible," "ain't you,
honey?" — they speak as she wrote them. Words that were ordinary in her day
and have since grown unfamiliar, such as *cateress*, *bier*, and
*gasolene*, are left as she used them. Every change made between her pages
and these is recorded, so that nothing she wrote is lost. Photographs of
all 288 original pages, the word-for-word transcription, and the full log
of edits are kept together online at
github.com/bprussell/alice-russell-manuscript.
